Acer's first gaming handheld sold officially in the US went on sale at Best Buy on November 1 for $1,299.99, and it has been hard to buy almost from day one. This Acer Predator Atlas 8 review pulls together the numbers from more than a dozen outlets, so you can decide whether Intel's new handheld chip is worth that money and the hunt for stock. The short version: it is the fastest Windows handheld you can get at this price, and it pays for that speed with weight and battery life.

Only three outlets published full reviews with their own testing: [PC Guide](https://www.pcguide.com/reviews/acer-predator-atlas-8/), [Noobfeed](https://www.noobfeed.com/articles/acer-predator-atlas-8-review-gaming-handheld), and Spain's [Profesional Review](https://www.profesionalreview.com/2026/11/02/acer-predator-atlas-8-review/). Tom's Guide and The Shortcut wrote hands-ons from pre-release demos, and several "tests" online are write-ups of YouTuber ETA Prime's videos.

## Quick answer

- The US model costs $1,299.99 at Best Buy, the only US retailer, with Intel's Arc G3 Extreme chip, 24GB of memory, and a 512GB SSD.
- It is fast. In ETA Prime's tests it ran Forza Horizon 6 above 70fps at 30 watts, where the $1,299.99 Asus ROG Xbox Ally X20 managed about 50fps at a lower resolution.
- Battery life is short in big games: about 1 hour 30 minutes of Cyberpunk 2077 in PC Guide's test.
- It is heavy. Acer says under 810g, and PC Guide's scale said 867g.
- The 8-inch screen is a sharp, bright LCD, not OLED. The Ally X20 has OLED at the same price.
- Stock is thin. Preorders sold out in October, and the listing was reported sold out again on November 2.
- Buy it for maximum speed per dollar, especially if you play near a charger or docked. Skip it if you want long unplugged sessions.

## Predator Atlas 8 price, stock, and the restock question

[Engadget reported](https://www.engadget.com/2298013/acer-predator-atlas-8-gaming-handheld-comes-to-best-buy-on-november-1-for-1300/) on October 27 that the Atlas 8 would reach the US on November 1 as a Best Buy exclusive, in one configuration: the Arc G3 Extreme model. [Make Tech Easier](https://maketecheasier.com/acer-predator-atlas-8-us-launch/) lists it with Arc B390 graphics, 24GB of memory, and a 512GB SSD. PC Guide's review mentions a $1,099 US price, but every US launch report we read describes only the $1,299.99 Extreme.

The Predator Atlas 8 price looks different in Britain. [GamingOnLinux reported UK pricing](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/07/acer-predator-atlas-8-handheld-with-intel-arc-g3-gets-uk-pricing-and-launch-month/) from £999.99 for the Arc G3 with 16GB, 512GB, and a 60Wh battery, up to £1,499.99 for the Extreme with 32GB and 1TB. The £1,299.99 Extreme gets 24GB, 1TB, and 80Wh. So the £999 headline figure buys a slower chip, and UK Extreme buyers get double the US storage.

Now stock. Best Buy started taking orders on October 23, according to IGN as cited by [shattered.io](https://shattered.io/acer-predator-atlas-8-best-buy-1299-99-us-launch-2026/), and that round sold out before launch, as [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/the-1300-acer-predator-atlas-8-handheld-is-finally-us-bound-and-its-pre-orders-are-already-sold-out/) reported. Acer told Wccftech stock would return November 1, [per MIXED](https://mixed-news.com/en/acer-predator-atlas-8-1299-sold-out-november-restock/). HandheldPicker then reported the listing sold out again on November 2. Shattered.io adds a caveat: Acer has published no unit counts, so a sellout says little about volume.

As of November 8 we found no announced date for the next Predator Atlas 8 restock. Check Best Buy's listing for model PA08-I51-G9Y7 and set a stock alert. Do not pay a reseller markup when rivals sell at the same $1,299.99.

## Performance: what Intel's Arc G3 Extreme can do

[Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/handheld-gaming/acer-brings-intel-arc-b390-graphics-to-predator-atlas-8-gaming-handheld-g3-extreme-cpu-paired-with-segment-first-metal-fan-for-increased-airflow) framed the Atlas 8 as Intel's challenge to AMD's dominance in handhelds. Profesional Review lists the Arc G3 Extreme as a 14-core chip on Intel's 18A process. Watts matter here: TDP is the power budget the chip may use, and more watts means more speed, more heat, and a faster-draining battery. PC Guide lists 25W for Quiet mode and 35W for Turbo.

The Predator Atlas 8 vs ROG Ally numbers are the headline. In ETA Prime's tests, [reported by Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Acer-Predator-Atlas-8-tested-Asus-ROG-Xbox-Ally-X20-stands-no-chance-despite-same-price.1435067.0.html), the Atlas 8 ran Forza Horizon 6 above 70fps at 1200p on medium with no upscaling at 30W, while the Ally X20 just passed 50fps at 1080p medium and 28W. In 3DMark Time Spy at 35W it scored 6,607 against 4,091 for the ROG Xbox Ally X. Notebookcheck called it "a superb gaming handheld," although MSI's Claw 8 EX AI+ with the same chip topped 7,000. ETA Prime also found Turbo mode misbehaving in Forza, which points to software Acer still needs to fix.

The hands-ons point the same way. [The Shortcut's Kevin Lee](https://www.theshortcut.com/p/acer-predator-atlas-8-hands-on-review) saw 55 to 59fps in Forza Horizon 6 at full resolution on high with XeSS, against up to 50fps on the ROG Xbox Ally X with AMD's FSR on the same preset. XeSS is Intel's upscaling: the game renders at a lower resolution and the chip rebuilds a sharper image, which buys frames. At Computex in June, [Tom's Guide's Jason England](https://www.tomsguide.com/gaming/handheld-gaming/acer-predator-atlas-8-hands-on-review) got 66fps at 1200p without ray tracing on the charger, 45fps at lower wattage, and 42fps with ultra settings, ray tracing, and XeSS.

The full reviews add more games. PC Guide, at native 1200p in Turbo, measured Cyberpunk 2077 at 56fps on Ultra and 80fps on Medium, and Quiet mode only dropped Medium to 73fps. Noobfeed averaged 75fps in Forza Horizon 6 on Low, but only 30 to 32fps in Crimson Desert on High. [Tom's Hardware's Ally X20 test](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/handheld-gaming/asus-rog-xbox-ally-x20-tested-same-performance-more-comfortable-and-a-glorious-oled-screen) sums up the gap for both Ally models: "Neither will play games as well as the Intel Arc G3."

## The 8-inch screen and the viewing-angle complaint

The display is an 8-inch, 1920 x 1200 IPS LCD touchscreen with a 120Hz refresh rate, variable refresh rate (VRR), and 500 nits of rated brightness. VRR lets the screen follow the game's changing frame rate, which prevents tearing and stutter.

Profesional Review's lab numbers back up the spec: 506 nits peak, 98.2% of sRGB, 74.4% of the wider DCI-P3 gamut, and a 1,494:1 contrast ratio, good for an IPS panel. A cool 7,732K color temperature means whites lean blue out of the box. Noobfeed called the image crisp, bright enough outdoors but not in direct sun.

The complaint came from The Shortcut's pre-production unit, which had "painfully narrow up and down viewing angles": tilting it about 20 degrees washed out roughly a quarter of the color. None of the three full reviews flagged this, and an early UK owner quoted by [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Acer-Predator-Atlas-8-First-units-arrive-initial-impressions-surface.1403487.0.html) rated the screen comparable to the ROG Xbox Ally X. If you game lying down with the screen tilted, try one in a store first.

OLED is the bigger issue. [Tom's Guide measured](https://www.tomsguide.com/gaming/handheld-gaming/asus-rog-xbox-ally-x20-review) the Ally X20's 7.4-inch OLED at 579.8 nits and 82.6% of DCI-P3, with the true blacks OLED is known for. If image quality matters more to you than frame rate, that is a real point against the Acer.

## Battery life, heat, and fan noise

The Extreme models carry an 80Wh battery, the same size as the ROG Xbox Ally X's. It does not stretch far. PC Guide ran Cyberpunk 2077 on Steam Deck settings at 50% brightness and got about 1 hour 30 minutes in Turbo and 1 hour 40 minutes in Quiet. Noobfeed got about 100 minutes in Turbo at full brightness, against just under two hours for the Claw 8 EX AI+ in the same conditions. Profesional Review measured about 1.3 hours at 40W and 2.2 hours at 25W, while video playback ran past 16 hours. Plan on a charger for any big game.

Cooling uses two fans, including what Acer calls the first metal fan in a gaming handheld, with 89 blades and a claimed 10% airflow gain over plastic. In PC Guide's stress test the GPU briefly hit 77°C, the grips stayed comfortable, and only the rear casing and top vents got hot. Turbo was noticeably louder than Quiet for a modest gain. Profesional Review measured 31 dBA at idle and 55 dBA at maximum performance, close to a gaming laptop, and listed the noise as a con. Next to someone sleeping, use Quiet mode or [noise-cancelling headphones](/blog/what-actually-matters-in-noise-cancelling-headphones/).

One bug to watch: the early UK owner said sleep mode left the fans running, though that is one unit, and an update could fix it.

## Controls, ports, and Windows 11

The sticks use TMR (tunnel magnetoresistance) sensors, a magnetic design that resists stick drift, the slow wandering that ruins older controllers. Engadget, PC Guide, Noobfeed, and Profesional Review all list TMR sticks. The Shortcut faulted its demo unit for lacking Hall-effect sticks but never mentioned TMR, a different magnetic sensor, so its unit may not have matched retail.

The triggers are Hall-effect, meaning magnets read the pull, so there are no contacts to wear out. A switch on the back turns them into short, click-like triggers for shooters or full analog travel for racing. Every review we read praised them.

The buttons split opinion. Tom's Guide found the D-pad and face buttons "a bit mushy" and preferred the Claw 8 EX AI+, and the UK owner agreed. PC Guide praised the tactile feedback, and Profesional Review called the gamepad excellent.

Ports are a strength: two Thunderbolt 4 ports, both with DisplayPort 2.1 and 65W charging per Profesional Review, plus a UHS-II microSD slot, a headphone jack, and Wi-Fi 7. In ETA Prime's docked tests, [summarized by Geeky Gadgets](https://www.geeky-gadgets.com/acer-predator-atlas-8-review/), it ran games at 1440p, and an unofficial 48W mode added 5 to 7% more speed. Acer ships a cardboard stand rather than a dock. For a one-cable desk, [our USB-C docking station guide](/blog/how-to-choose-a-usb-c-docking-station-for-a-laptop/) covers power and display support, [our Thunderbolt 5 dock explainer](/blog/thunderbolt-5-docks-explained-bandwidth-boost-dual-4k-pcie-storage/) covers bandwidth, and [our 24 vs 27-inch monitor guide](/blog/24-vs-27-inch-monitor-at-60-cm-on-a-shallow-desk/) helps you pick the screen.

It runs Windows 11 Home with Xbox's full-screen experience on by default, a console-style home screen that, as Tom's Hardware explains, skips loading parts of Windows to save memory. PC Guide found first setup took many updates and some apps displayed poorly, but you can pin Steam to the Xbox home screen. Acer's PredatorSense app handles performance modes, fans, and lighting.

## Where reviewers disagree

- **Battery.** Tom's Guide saw only a 5% drop in 20 minutes off the charger in its Computex demo. The full reviews measured about 90 to 100 minutes in demanding games.
- **Screen.** The Shortcut called the viewing angles terrible on early hardware. Noobfeed and Profesional Review liked the retail display.
- **Buttons.** Tom's Guide and the UK owner found them mushy. PC Guide and Profesional Review rated them highly.
- **Weight.** Acer and Profesional Review list 810g, Noobfeed lists 860g, PC Guide measured 867g, and the UK owner 870g.
- **Noise.** The UK owner called the fans quiet. Profesional Review measured 55 dBA and called them loud.
- **Overall.** PC Guide gave it 4.5 out of 5 and Profesional Review 90%. Noobfeed gave no score and called it "a good first attempt."

## Who should buy it, and who should skip

Buy it if you want the most gaming speed $1,299.99 can buy, play new, demanding games, or plan to dock it. On the published numbers it has a fair claim to being the best Windows gaming handheld for raw performance at its price.

Get the ROG Xbox Ally X20 instead if screen quality and comfort matter more than frame rates, or the $999.99 ROG Xbox Ally X for AMD's performance at $300 less. Get a Steam Deck OLED if you mostly play older or indie games and want something light. If you mostly game on your phone, a fan-cooled phone like [the RedMagic 10 Pro](/blog/redmagic-10-pro-review-7050mah-silicon-battery-active-fan-snapdragon-8-elite/) costs far less.

Wait if you want a smaller Intel Arc handheld. Engadget says the 7-inch Predator Atlas 7 uses the same G3 Extreme chip, and shattered.io reports Acer plans it for North America in the fourth quarter of 2026, with no US price yet.

## Acer Predator Atlas 8 review: the verdict

Acer built a fast, well-cooled Intel Arc handheld with excellent triggers, TMR sticks, and two full Thunderbolt 4 ports. ETA Prime's tests put it well ahead of the $1,299.99 Ally X20, and all three full reviews came out positive. The costs are real, though: roughly 90 minutes of battery in heavy games, a body that weighed about 60g over Acer's claim on two scales, and an LCD where the same money buys OLED. If performance is your priority and you can find one at $1,299.99, buy it. If you want a handheld for long flights, the Ally X20 or a Steam Deck OLED fits better.
