Noise cancelling is one of the most oversold features in consumer audio. Adverts promise silence, and
what you actually get depends entirely on which sounds you are trying to remove. Understanding that
difference is the difference between a satisfying purchase and a disappointing one.

## Noise cancelling is frequency specific

Active noise cancelling works best on steady, low-frequency sound. Engine drone, air conditioning,
and the hum of a train are its natural targets. It is much weaker against sudden or high-pitched
sounds such as a nearby conversation, a barking dog, or a dropped mug. No headphone removes those
completely, and any claim to the contrary is marketing.

## The number on the box means little

Decibel reduction figures are measured under specific conditions that rarely match your room. Treat
them as a rough ranking at best. What matters more is how the headphones handle the frequencies you
actually face, which is why a model that excels on a plane may do little for an open office.

## Comfort decides whether you use them

A great pair that hurts after an hour is a waste of money. Look at weight, clamping force, and whether
the earpads can be replaced. Over-ear designs seal better and distribute pressure, while on-ear
models are lighter but press on the ear. Try before buying if you can, and check that replacement
pads are available, because worn pads quietly ruin both comfort and sound.

## Passive isolation is half the story

A good seal blocks a surprising amount of noise before any electronics are involved. Deep eartips or
well-padded cups can outperform a fancy chip with a poor fit. This is why fit matters as much as the
specification sheet.

## Features that genuinely help

Multipoint pairing, which keeps a laptop and a phone connected at once, is worth more than a slightly
lower noise figure. A clear microphone matters if you take calls. Long battery life matters if you
travel. Transparency mode, which pipes the outside world back in, is useful on a commute and at a
desk. Check how each behaves before you commit.

## Sound and codecs

Codec support only matters if your devices support the same codec. For most listeners, fit and tuning
matter far more than the bitrate of the wireless link. Look for a sound signature you enjoy rather
than one that measures best.

## How to decide

Rank your three most common listening situations and pick the headphones that handle those best. If
you fly, prioritise low-frequency cancelling and battery life. If you share a home office, prioritise
comfort, a good microphone, and multipoint. If you mostly listen in quiet, spend the money on sound
and build quality instead. Honest priorities beat any single number.
