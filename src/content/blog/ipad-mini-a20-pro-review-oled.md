Apple's first OLED iPad mini went on sale November 6, and the reviews landed on November 3. This iPad mini A20 Pro review pulls together the lab numbers and opinions from Engadget, Macworld, Tom's Guide, Six Colors, MacStories, The Verge, and more, so you can decide whether the new mini earns its $699 price. The short version: reviewers love the screen and the speed, nearly all of them knock the 60Hz refresh rate, and the starting price is $200 higher than the last mini's at launch.

## Quick answer

- It starts at $699 for 128GB on Wi-Fi, $200 above the A17 Pro mini's 2024 launch price and $100 above what it cost after Apple's June 2026 increase.
- The 8.5-inch OLED screen is the big upgrade. Apple rates it at 1,200 nits peak HDR, and Tom's Guide measured 1,032 nits.
- It is still 60Hz. Nearly every review flags it, and The Verge says the Apple Pencil looks jittery because of it.
- Speed jumped. Tom's Guide's unit scored 4,720 single-core in Geekbench 6, against 2,883 for the old mini.
- Battery life beats the rating: Tom's Guide got 14 hours 24 minutes of browsing against Apple's 10-hour claim.
- The smart buy is the $799 256GB model. Skip the $999 512GB tier unless you need the space or run AI models on the device.

## What $699 buys: prices, configs, and the 5-core catch

Apple announced the iPad mini (A20 Pro) on October 26. [Apple's newsroom post](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-gives-ipad-mini-its-biggest-update-ever/) lists $699 for Wi-Fi and $849 for Wi-Fi + Cellular, with education pricing from $649. Storage comes in 128GB, 256GB, and 512GB at $699, $799, and $999, per [iThinkDiff's review roundup](https://www.ithinkdiff.com/oled-ipad-mini-review-roundup/), and cellular adds $150 to each. Many outlets call it the iPad mini 8th generation, or iPad mini 8.

The "$200 more" headline needs context. The A17 Pro mini launched at $499, rose to $599 in June 2026, and was discontinued when the new model arrived, per [MacRumors' buyer's guide](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ipad-mini-2024-vs-2026/) and [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/03/new-ipad-mini-a20-pro-review/).

Nano-texture glass, an etched matte finish that cuts glare, adds $100 and only comes on the 256GB and 512GB models. Six Colors' fully loaded review unit (512GB, cellular, nano-texture) cost $1,249.

Now the catch. [Apple's spec sheet](https://support.apple.com/en-me/149482) gives the 128GB and 256GB models a 5-core A20 Pro: two "super" cores, three efficiency cores, and one 16-core Neural Engine. Only the 512GB model gets the 6-core chip with a fourth efficiency core and a dual 16-core Neural Engine. All three share a 7-core GPU.

Every test unit we could identify was the 512GB 6-core model, so nobody has published numbers for the cheaper chip yet. [Engadget's Nathan Ingraham](https://www.engadget.com/2304616/apple-ipad-mini-a20-pro-review/) couldn't compare the two but thinks most buyers will be fine with the 5-core version, and 9to5Mac says not to buy 512GB just for the extra core. Since the missing core is an efficiency core, everyday speed should be close. Get the $799 256GB model. 9to5Mac calls 128GB tight, HardwareZone found iPadOS and system data take about 18GB (per iThinkDiff), and 256GB is the cheapest way into nano-texture.

## The OLED iPad mini screen: brighter, inkier, still 60Hz

The old mini used an LCD, a layer of liquid crystals lit by a backlight that never fully switches off, so black looks dark gray. OLED pixels make their own light and turn off completely for black.

Apple lists an 8.5-inch panel, up from 8.3 inches thanks to 24 percent thinner bezels. Resolution is 2316 by 1512 at 326 ppi, with 600 nits for everyday content, 1,200 nits peak for HDR, and a 1-nit minimum for reading in the dark. Tom's Guide measured 600 nits SDR and 1,032 nits HDR with 40 percent of the screen lit, per [NeoTeo's summary of its review](https://www.neoteo.com/en/ipad-mini-8-review-oled-benchmarks-and-60hz-limits). It is a standard OLED, not the iPad Pro's tandem OLED, per 9to5Mac.

The refresh rate is the sore spot. ProMotion is Apple's name for screens that redraw up to 120 times a second, which makes scrolling and pen strokes look smoother. The mini stays at 60Hz, while the iPhone Duo, which uses the same A20 Pro, gets ProMotion. Apple's argument, [per MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/03/ipad-mini-8-review-highlights-lack-of-120hz/), is that OLED pixels respond so much faster than LCD that motion blur drops anyway. The Verge's David Pierce isn't convinced: the Pencil looks more jittery and scrolling text blurs, though he calls the panel "still a big upgrade overall." [Six Colors' Jason Snell](https://sixcolors.com/post/2026/11/ipad-mini-review-a-modern-multitasker/) didn't mind 60Hz at all.

One gap in the coverage: flicker. Most OLED screens dim by switching pixels on and off very fast, a method called PWM (pulse-width modulation), and low-frequency PWM gives some people headaches. None of the reviews we read measured the mini's PWM rate. If you're sensitive, try one in a store first, and see [our guide to reducing eye strain](/blog/reducing-eye-strain-at-a-desk-distance-lighting-breaks/).

## A20 Pro benchmarks vs the A17 Pro mini and the iPad Air

Apple claims up to 60 percent faster multithreaded CPU performance and up to 2.7 times faster graphics than the A17 Pro mini. Testers landed on both sides of that claim.

Tom's Guide's 512GB unit scored 4,720 single-core and 12,697 multi-core in Geekbench 6, against 2,883 and 7,213 for the A17 Pro mini, per NeoTeo. The 11-inch M4 iPad Air scored 3,745 and 13,342. That puts the mini about 26 percent ahead of the Air on single-core work and about 5 percent behind when every core is busy. In 3DMark Wild Life Extreme, a graphics test, the mini hit 9,119 points against 6,730 for the Air and 3,001 for the old mini.

[Macworld's Roman Loyola](https://www.macworld.com/article/3273892/ipad-mini-a20-pro-review.html) measured smaller gains over the A17 Pro: 51 percent single-core, 50 percent multi-core, and 180 percent on the GPU.

Real work backs up the charts. Engadget ran Control and No Man's Sky without hitches and found Lightroom RAW edits near-instant. [MacStories' John Voorhees](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/ipad-mini-2026-review-pushing-the-boundaries-with-apples-smallest-ipad/) ran a 4-billion-parameter Qwen language model at about 40 tokens per second, faster than an 11-inch M4 iPad Pro in its tests. Six Colors says the mini is more powerful than a MacBook Neo, the [$699 Mac we reviewed](/blog/macbook-neo-review-home-office/) for home offices.

## Battery life: smaller battery, longer runtime

The battery shrank slightly, from 19.3Wh to 18.9Wh, and Apple's rating stays at 10 hours of Wi-Fi browsing or video. Testers mostly beat it:

- Tom's Guide: 14 hours 24 minutes of continuous Wi-Fi browsing at 150 nits, against 10 hours 36 minutes for the old mini. It managed one run instead of its usual three, per [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/03/ipad-mini-8-review-finds-longer-battery-life/).
- Macworld: 10 hours 10 minutes of looped video at 200 nits.
- [Man of Many](https://manofmany.com/tech/apple-ipad-mini-review-2026): 9.5 to 10 hours of mixed use on Wi-Fi, and about 8.5 hours on 5G outdoors.
- The Verge: about 9 hours of everyday use, per iThinkDiff.
- Engadget: 5 to 6 hours of heavy use.

Charging got faster. Apple promises 50 percent in about 30 minutes with a 35W or larger adapter (not included), and Engadget went from 29 to 74 percent in 30 minutes on a 40W charger. Plan on a full day of reading and browsing, and about half that if you game.

## Design, IP67, speakers, and Apple Pencil support

The new mini is 5.4mm thick and weighs 255g on Wi-Fi (263g with cellular), down from 6.3mm and 293g. It is also the first iPad with an official water rating, IP67, which Apple tests at up to 1 meter for 30 minutes. Pierce says that changed where he uses it, from the kitchen to the shower, per iThinkDiff.

To seal the body, Apple removed the speaker holes and built an "Acoustic Display," with tweeters and woofers hidden behind the glass. The 12MP Center Stage front camera moved to the long edge, so you're centered on video calls with the mini held sideways. Unlocking still uses Touch ID in the top button, with no Face ID, and the only colors are midnight and starlight.

The Apple Pencil Pro ($129) and the USB-C Apple Pencil ($79) both work, with hover. There's no Magic Keyboard for the mini, Man of Many notes, so typing means a Bluetooth keyboard.

USB-C runs at 10Gb/s and drives one external display at up to 4K and 60Hz. Apple says iPadOS 27.2 adds extended display mode, so a monitor can show different apps instead of mirroring the mini.

## Where the iPhone Duo fits

Apple's other small big-screen device is the iPhone Duo foldable. [Apple's spec sheet](https://www.apple.com/iphone-duo/specs/) lists a 7.6-inch inner screen with 120Hz ProMotion, the same A20 Pro chip, and 254g, one gram less than the Wi-Fi mini. It starts at $1,999 for 256GB, $1,300 more than the mini. It only works with the USB-C Apple Pencil, without hover, while the mini takes the Pencil Pro.

Opinions split. [Macworld's David Price](https://www.macworld.com/article/3270413/apples-new-ipad-mini-is-a-duo-you-cant-close.html), writing at announcement, called the Duo "just straight up better" head to head. Man of Many pitches the mini as the sensible alternative to the A$3,499 Duo. The Duo makes sense if you want one device in your pocket. For a dedicated reading and notes screen, the mini is the smarter $1,300 decision, and early Duo owners have hit hinge problems covered in [our iPhone Duo hinge crack guide](/blog/iphone-duo-hinge-crack-screen-problem/).

## Where reviewers disagree

- **60Hz.** The Verge says the difference shows even in basic swiping, and HardwareZone calls it a "serious flaw," per iThinkDiff. Gizmodo argues most people won't notice the missing 120Hz, per the same roundup.
- **Speakers.** Engadget heard stronger bass, and [TechRadar's French edition](https://global.techradar.com/fr-fr/tablets/ipad-mini/test-de-lipad-mini-2026-apple-passe-enfin-a-loled-et-le-resultat-impressionne) calls them particularly impressive. Macworld could barely tell them from the old mini's, and Six Colors says the iPad can vibrate at high volume.
- **Nano-texture.** Engadget and TechRadar like it, while Gizmodo says it dulled the OLED's colors and would skip it, per iThinkDiff. Snell lost some of the deepest blacks.
- **Price.** The Verge says $699 feels at least $100 too high, per iThinkDiff. Engadget wrote, "I'm not sure if the price will be worth it for most." Snell calls it a "full-featured product at a full-featured price."

## Who should buy it: is the iPad mini worth it for you

For three groups, yes. If you own a sixth-generation mini or older, [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3273892/ipad-mini-a20-pro-review.html) says upgrade, and you gain Apple Intelligence support. If you read and take notes, the 1-nit minimum brightness and Pencil Pro support make this the best small iPad yet; Voorhees found reading apps "a delight." And if your tablet lives in a bag, a kitchen, or by a pool, 255g and IP67 matter more than any benchmark.

Skip it if your A17 Pro mini still works fine. MacRumors sees no strong reason to upgrade for speed alone, so the screen has to be worth $699 to you. Skip it if you want a laptop replacement, since the $749 iPad Air takes a Magic Keyboard. Skip it if you mostly read books: Six Colors points out Kindles top out around $320 and the Kobo Libra Colour costs $260, and [our Kindle Colorsoft 2026 review](/blog/kindle-colorsoft-2026-review-color-e-reader/) covers the color e-ink option. If you draw or game a lot, try the 60Hz screen in a store before you buy.

## iPad mini A20 Pro review: the verdict

Apple called this the iPad mini's biggest update ever, and reviewers mostly agree. The OLED screen, a chip that beats the iPad Air on single-core speed, IP67, and 38 fewer grams add up to what The Verge calls the best iPad mini yet (per iThinkDiff), and Trusted Reviews calls it "agonisingly close to perfection," per [IBTimes AU](https://www.ibtimes.com.au/apple-ipad-mini-oled-a20-pro-review-1877977). The two flaws are real: a 60Hz screen on a $699 tablet, and a starting price $200 above the 2024 launch. Buy the 256GB model at $799, skip nano-texture unless you read outdoors, and leave the 512GB tier to people who need the storage or run AI models locally. A17 Pro owners can wait unless the OLED screen is the thing they wanted.
