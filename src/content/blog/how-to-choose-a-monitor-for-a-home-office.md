Most people buy a monitor by choosing a size and a budget, then discover a month later that the stand
does not rise high enough or that text looks soft. A monitor tends to stay on your desk for years, so
it is worth deciding what you need before you open a single product page.

## Start with the work you do

A person reading code, a person editing photos, and a person running spreadsheets need different
things. High text clarity matters most for reading and writing. Wide colour coverage matters for
photo and video work. Vertical space matters for spreadsheets and long documents. Write down your two
or three most common tasks and let them drive the shortlist.

## Resolution and screen size travel together

The number of pixels matters less than how tightly they are packed. A large screen at a low
resolution looks grainy, and a small screen at a very high resolution makes text tiny unless the
operating system scales it. Pick a size and resolution combination that keeps text comfortable
without scaling, because scaling adds blur in some applications and always spends performance.

## Panel type decides the character of the image

The three common panel families each trade something away. One offers deep blacks and wide viewing
angles but can dim unevenly and may be slower to respond. Another is fast and bright but has weaker
contrast. The cheapest type is fine for office work and poor for colour-critical work. None is simply
best, so match the panel to the task rather than to the price.

## The stand matters more than the specifications

A monitor you cannot position comfortably will hurt your neck no matter how good the picture is. Look
for height adjustment, tilt, and ideally rotation. If the stand is fixed, check whether it uses a
standard mounting pattern so you can add an arm later. Many attractive monitors ship with a stand
that barely moves.

## Ports, cables, and charging

Count the devices you need to connect and add one spare. A single cable that carries video, data, and
laptop charging is a genuine convenience, so check the power it can deliver and whether your laptop
supports it. Cables in the box are often short, so budget for a longer one.

## What to ignore

Very high refresh rates are pleasant for games but rarely change office work. Extremely high
brightness matters only in a sunlit room. Curved screens help mainly at very large sizes. Do not pay
for a specification that will not change your day.

## A simple decision order

Size first, then resolution, then panel type, then the stand, then ports. If two options are equal on
those points, choose the cheaper one and put the savings into an adjustable arm or a better chair.
That order keeps the money where you will feel it every day.

Buying a monitor is really an exercise in knowing your own habits. Get that right and almost any
reasonable model will serve you for years. Get it wrong and the most expensive screen in the shop
will still be the wrong one.
