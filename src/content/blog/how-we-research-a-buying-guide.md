Most review sites are not complicated. Someone gets a product, spends a few hours with it, writes
about the parts that photograph well, and moves on. That is fine for a first impression. It is a poor
basis for advice you might spend real money on.

We try to do something simpler and slower: work out what matters before we look at any product, then
check candidates against that list.

## Start with the decision, not the product

The first question is never "which one is best". It is "what is this person actually trying to do".
A keyboard for writing is a different problem from a keyboard for gaming, and a recommendation that
ignores that difference is just a list of expensive things.

So every guide starts with the axes that change the answer. For a monitor that might be panel type,
resolution, refresh rate, and whether the stand height-adjusts. For a laptop it might be battery life
under a realistic load rather than the number on the box. Once the axes are clear, most products
eliminate themselves.

## Read the specifications properly

Marketing pages describe intentions. Specification sheets and manuals describe behaviour. We read the
manual, the datasheet, and the compatibility notes, because that is where the limits hide: the port
that is not full speed, the adapter that is sold separately, the feature that only works on one
platform.

## Check what owners report

A short review tells you how a product feels on day one. A six-month owner report tells you what
wears out. We look for patterns rather than single complaints, and we treat a repeated failure across
many reviews as a real risk even when the average rating looks healthy.

## Check the price history

A discount is only a discount if the price was actually higher before. We look at the price over
time, so we can tell you whether today is a good day to buy or whether you should wait.

## Write the recommendation we would follow

Finally we pick. Usually that means one main recommendation, a cheaper alternative, and a reason to
avoid the rest. If nothing is worth buying, we say that too, and the guide waits until something is.
