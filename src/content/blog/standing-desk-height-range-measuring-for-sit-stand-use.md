## Quick answer

- Seated, the desk has to reach 71–76 cm (28–30 in) to match a standard desk. 74 cm (29 in) is the common default.
- Standing, set the surface so your elbows sit near 90 degrees with your shoulders relaxed. That is a body measurement, not a spec-sheet number.
- The quoted band for adjustable desks is 61–127 cm (24–50 in). That describes the market, not your model, so check the actual minimum and maximum.
- A 76 cm (30 in) deep top keeps a monitor inside OSHA's stated 50–100 cm (20–40 in) viewing distance.

Most sit-stand shoppers compare motors, presets and lift speed. The two numbers that decide whether a desk fits are buried at the bottom of the listing: minimum and maximum height. A frame that will not drop to your seated height leaves you on a raised chair with your feet off the floor. One that stops short of your standing height leaves your wrists cocked upward all afternoon.

Here is how to find both numbers for your body, and what else to measure before you order.

## What height range does a sit-stand desk need to cover?

The table shows the reference heights that matter when a sit-stand desk is sized against a real body.

| Reference point | Height (cm) | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common fixed desk | 71–76 | seated baseline to match |
| Common default | 74 | middle of that band |
| Quoted adjustable band | 61–127 | market range, verify per model |
| Low seated surface | 66 | edge of the 5'2"–5'10" band |

The first two rows are the practical test. If you are replacing a desk that already works, the new one has to match it when seated. The 66 cm figure sits at the top of the range for people around 5'2" to 5'6" and at the bottom of the range for 5'6" to 5'10", so it is the number to chase if a standard 74 cm top has always felt slightly high. Above roughly 5'10", the same guidance points back to the 71–76 cm band, or to an adjustable desk with enough travel.

The 61–127 cm band describes the market, not any one desk. Verify the minimum and maximum on the model you want rather than assuming a range.

## How do you measure your standing height at home?

The rule that sets standing height is a body check, not a number from a spec sheet: elbows at about 90 degrees with the shoulders relaxed. Progressive Desk publishes that rule, and it applies at any body height.

The practical version is to stand in your work shoes, let your arms hang, and measure from the floor to the crease at the back of your elbow. That is roughly where the surface should land, because it is the height at which your forearms sit level with your shoulders relaxed.

The measurement is a starting point, not a setting. Your keyboard adds height: if the keys sit above the surface, a desk set exactly at your elbow crease leaves the forearms angled slightly up.

The seated version of the check is short: feet flat on the floor, knees at about 90 degrees, forearms parallel to the floor, wrists straight, monitor at or just below eye level. ISO 9241-5:2024, the standard for workstation layout and postural requirements, covers the same arrangement of screens, desks, chairs and controls, including sit-stand use and multiple displays. Our guide to [desk height and keyboard position](/blog/desk-height-and-keyboard-position-setting-up-for-comfort/) walks through the sequence.

When the desk's lowest position sits above your seated elbow height, a keyboard tray is the cheapest correction. The VIVO KB05E keyboard tray clamps under the top and gives the keying surface a height of its own, which is the layer that has to hit the 90-degree mark.

## Why the lowest setting matters more than the highest

The bottom of the range is the setting you return to every time you sit; the top is used in stretches. That makes the minimum the more consequential figure.

Run the arithmetic with your own body. If your seated elbow height is 63 cm and the desk stops at 71 cm, you spend the seated half of the day with your shoulders shrugged. The fixes are all visible: raise the chair and your feet leave the floor unless you add a footrest, move the keyboard onto a tray, or buy a frame with a lower minimum.

At the top, the logic reverses. If your standing elbow crease measures 112 cm, a frame that tops out at 110 cm gives you no workable setting, and no memory preset rescues that.

Whatever minimum and maximum you end up needing, check how the frame holds itself when it is fully raised. Our breakdown of [standing desk stability](/blog/standing-desk-stability-what-to-check-before-buying/) covers what to test.

## How much room does the desk need around it?

The table lists the non-height dimensions worth checking before you order.

| Dimension | Figure (cm) |
| --- | --- |
| Knee clearance width | 61 min |
| Width for two monitors | 152 |
| Preferred depth | 76 |
| Clearance behind desk | 91–122 |

Depth decides whether the monitor can sit far enough away. OSHA's stated viewing distance is 50–100 cm (20–40 in), and a 76 cm top gives a monitor on its own stand room to sit in the far half of that range. On a shallower top, a large panel on its factory foot often ends up closer than the 50 cm minimum, which is tiring across a full day, and a desk mount is usually cheaper than a deeper desk. The single monitor arm clamps to the back edge so the panel hangs over the rear of the desk instead of on it. Check the plate pattern against your monitor first, which our [VESA mount compatibility guide](/blog/vesa-mount-compatibility-plate-sizes-and-screw-patterns/) explains.

Knee clearance is the constraint people find too late. The listed minimum knee clearance width is 61 cm (24 in), and that space has to stay clear of frame legs, drawer stacks and any cable tray hung low underneath.

Two measurements come before a wide top: 91 to 122 cm of clear floor behind the desk so the chair can roll, and the doorframe. A top wider than 152 cm may have to be assembled in the room it lives in, because it will not pass through a door as one piece.

Two smaller details change with height. Leave slack in the cables so full travel does not pull a plug out of the strip, and clamp the task light to the desk rather than standing it on a shelf behind, so it rises with the surface instead of raking across the screen at a new angle. The 2250LM clamp desk lamp mounts at the edge for that reason.

## Does the adjustment mechanism change what you should buy?

WorkSafe Victoria's office layout guidance says the adjustment mechanism itself should not create a manual handling risk, and names electric adjustment as the most appropriate option. It also says multi-user workstations must adjust to meet the needs of different users, a hard ask of a crank wound against the weight of the top.

Certification is the other half of the question, and it is widely misread. ANSI and BIFMA are not government agencies, and BIFMA participation is voluntary, so a desk with no named standard is not breaking a rule, only leaving you without evidence. When a maker claims testing, the useful detail is which standard. BIFMA X5.5 covers the safety, durability and structural performance of desk and table products intended for commercial offices, and a product has to pass every test defined in a standard to be labelled as certified. There are no partial certifications. On a powered desk, the electrical components also have to be tested to UL standards.

Durability testing is real work. An independent lab cycles a sit-stand desk thousands of times over one to three weeks, depending on the motor's duty cycle. Most retail and online sellers, with rare exceptions, do not pay for it. If no standard is named, ask what was run.

For a motorised frame, the HUANUO electric standing desk is one option, but the comparison only becomes useful once the maker puts the minimum and maximum height in writing. Two frames that look alike online can differ at the bottom by more than your seated position can absorb.

## What should you confirm before you order?

- Get the minimum and maximum height in writing, with units, before you buy.
- Compare the minimum against your seated elbow height, and against 71–76 cm if you are replacing a desk that works.
- Compare the maximum against your standing elbow crease, measured in your work shoes.
- Confirm at least 61 cm of knee clearance width under the top, including anything mounted below it.
- Decide depth against your monitor: 76 cm for two screens or a large panel, otherwise budget for a monitor arm.
- Check 91–122 cm of clear floor behind the desk, and measure the doorframe if the top is wider than 152 cm.
- Ask which standard is named on the certification, and on a powered desk whether the electrics are UL tested.
- Budget for what the height range does not cover: a footrest, a keyboard tray, a monitor arm, a clamp lamp.
