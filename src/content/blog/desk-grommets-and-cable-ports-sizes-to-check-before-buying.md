## Quick answer

- Measure the hole diameter across its widest point. It is the reference dimension on a grommet size chart.
- Measure desktop thickness at the cut edge. One manufacturer's snap-in pass-through range grips 0.8 mm to 6.0 mm.
- Measure the cable bundle and any connector head, not one cable.
- Some solid wood tops ship with 80 mm holes, a size that accepts a wide selection of grommet-mounted power modules.
- Published charts are typical values. Confirm tolerances with the supplier before ordering.

A grommet is a ring or sleeve that lines a hole so the cut edge cannot saw into a cable. It is a small part that fails for dull reasons: the buyer treats the desk hole as one number, orders against it, and then finds the ring will not seat, will not grip, or squeezes the cable it was meant to protect.

This guide works through the three dimensions that have to agree, then the shape, material, and desk-damage questions that decide between a plain edge cover and a grommet-mounted power module.

## What three measurements decide whether a grommet fits?

The table sets out the three dimensions that have to work together, and where each one is measured.

| Dimension | What it controls | Where to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Panel hole diameter | Whether the grommet seats or sits loose | Across the widest point of the hole |
| Panel thickness (groove range) | Whether the grommet grips at installation | The desk edge inside the cut-out |
| Inner diameter | Whether the cable passes or is pinched | Against the cable bundle |

Grommet sizing guides are direct about it: proper sizing depends on the panel hole diameter, the panel thickness, and the inner diameter for the passing component. Get two right and the third wrong, and the part either will not install or will not last.

Hole diameter is normally the primary reference dimension. Too small, and the grommet will not seat or will tear as you press it in. Too large, and it sits loose enough to fall out. The groove is the channel around the outside that snaps over the panel edge, and it is cut for a narrow thickness range; ignoring panel thickness is a common cause of installation failure, mostly because buyers never think to measure it. The bore, or inner diameter, is the passage the cable occupies. Too small and it compresses and can damage the cable. Too large and the cable is free to move and vibrate.

## What size hole does your desk already have?

Most desk makers do not give buyers options around the diameter of their grommet holes. Diameter and position come with the top, so the usual job is fitting a part to a hole you did not choose. Measure across the widest point with calipers or a steel rule; a soft tape measure is awkward to hold across a circle, and on a small hole a millimetre or two decides whether a ring seats or drops through. Record both axes if the cut-out is oval.

Grommet-mounted power and USB modules come in a few standard diameters, so the module has to match the hole. Some solid wood tops ship with 80 mm holes, a size that accepts a wide selection of power management devices, which makes it the more useful hole if power at the desktop is the point. If you are routing mains power rather than a data cable, plan that side first, including where the power strip sits relative to the desk edge, as covered in the guide to [surge protection for a desk](/blog/surge-protection-for-a-desk-power-planning-and-what-to-buy/).

## Does the grommet grip the thickness of your desktop?

Snap-in grommets are specified against an edge wall thickness range, not a single number. HellermannTyton lists its cable pass-through grommets for edges from 0.8 mm to 6.0 mm, in gray and black. If your desktop falls outside the band a product family is sold for, it will not grip, however well the hole diameter matches.

Shape matters as much. A round snap-in grommet assumes a round hole. For larger or unshaped cut-outs with sharp or straight edges, the same manufacturer points to grommet strips, edging that presses onto the cut edge, rather than a ring.

## Round or oval: which shape fits the cut-out?

Round is the usual format, and it suits the round cables most desks carry. Oblong or oval pass-through grommets are made for flat cables and ribbon cables, which a circular port can pinch. If you run a flat display cable or a ribbon, check both axes of the opening before ordering a round part.

Bore sizing is where near-misses happen. Buyers measure the cable and forget the connector on its end, usually the widest thing that has to travel through the hole. Bundles are the other trap, because cables do not lie perfectly parallel and the group takes more room than the sum of their diameters.

Two accessories get confused with each other. Grommets fitted to cable ends relieve strain. Anti-kink sleeves prevent bending or kinking, a different failure. Some products combine both, so decide which problem you have.

## Which material and rating matter?

Snap-in cable grommets are made in PVC or flame-retardant PVC-FR, so the material grade belongs in the specification next to the diameter. The two look identical in a product photo.

A UL 94 rating is one of twelve defined flame classifications produced by small-scale flame tests, not a general fire-safety claim. Ratings are not interchangeable across test methods: a VTM-0 rating on thin film is not equivalent to V-0, and the same applies between VTM-1 or VTM-2 and V-1 or V-2. When a seller presents a VTM rating as though it were a V rating, treat the specification as unverified.

Temperature is worth checking too. Slotted grommet edging in a standard PE grade is listed at minus 30 to plus 80 degrees Celsius, which covers ordinary indoor conditions.

## Does the hole protect the desk or damage it?

On the vast majority of desks the grommet hole exposes the raw core of the tabletop, usually particleboard or MDF. The cap, ring, or cover is typically plastic or rubber, and its job is to stop cables being cut by a sharp edge. It does not seal the exposed core, so it offers little against long-term humidity and temperature swings, and it will not save a particleboard core from a spill that pools at the hole.

Better tops finish the hole itself. 3D-laminated tabletops are described as creating a hermetic seal over the full depth of the grommet hole rather than covering it, which is a construction difference you buy, not an accessory you add later. If you are still choosing a top, the guide to [desk surface materials](/blog/desk-surface-materials-laminate-vs-veneer-vs-solid-wood/) explains how laminate, veneer, and solid wood behave at a cut edge.

## What should you check before you order?

- Measure the hole diameter across its widest point, with calipers or a steel rule.
- Measure desktop thickness at the edge inside the hole, not at the outer edge of the desk.
- Compare that thickness against the grommet's stated grip or groove range before comparing diameters.
- Measure the widest item that must pass through: a connector head, or the bundle you expect to run in a year.
- Match shape to the cut-out. Round hole, round grommet. Slot or irregular shape, grommet strip.
- Decide the single job you need done: edge protection, strain relief, anti-kink protection, or power at the surface.
- Confirm the material grade and any UL 94 class, and do not accept a VTM rating as a substitute for a V rating.
- Ask the supplier to confirm tolerances in writing. Published charts are typical values, not guarantees.

## If the free fixes are not enough

Measurement solves most grommet problems. The table maps the situations that need a purchase to the kind of product that answers each.

| Situation | What answers it |
|---|---|
| Cables fray where they cross the cut edge | Plastic or rubber ring or hole cover (edge guard only) |
| Hole is an odd diameter, a long slot, or irregular | Grommet strip or edging, sized to the opening |
| Desktop thickness outside a snap-in grommet's grip band | Grommet or edging rated for the measured panel thickness |
| Mains power and USB wanted at the desk surface | Grommet-mounted power module matching the existing hole |

Whichever way you go, the measurements come first: hole diameter, panel thickness, and the widest thing that has to pass through.
