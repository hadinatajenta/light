Cable management is usually sold as a box of accessories. That is the last step, not the first. The reason a desk looks messy is almost never a lack of clips. It is that the power, the routing, and the cable lengths were never planned, so every cable has to cross the desk to reach something.

This guide covers the order to fix things in, which parts are worth buying, and the mistakes that make a tidy desk come apart the first time you move something.

## Fix power before anything else

Every cable problem starts at the socket.

If there is one socket on the wrong side of the room, every device on the desk has to reach it, and long cables cross everything. Before buying a single clip, decide where power should be:

- **A socket near or under the desk**, ideally more than one, so monitors and a charger do not share a single strip.
- **A trailing socket mounted to the desk**, so it moves with the desk if the desk is adjustable. A socket on the floor plus a rising desk equals a stretched or unplugged cable.
- **Separate the high draw items** from sensitive ones. A monitor and a laptop charger on one strip is normal. A space heater on the same strip is not.

Only once power is settled does the rest of the plan make sense.

## Route once, in a single direction

The second most common mistake is routing cables in two directions. Some go left to the power, some go right to the laptop, and they cross in the middle where you can see them.

Pick one direction, usually along the back edge of the desk, and bring everything to it. Then decide how each group leaves the desk:

- **Display cables** travel together to the monitor or the dock.
- **Power cables** travel together to the socket or strip.
- **Charging cables** for devices you actually move during the day are the exception. They should stay loose and reachable, not bundled.

A cable that you unplug daily should never be inside a bundle. You will open the bundle, and it will never close again.

## Length is a specification, not an afterthought

Cables are almost always too long or too short, and both cause mess.

Too long means slack that has to go somewhere, and slack is what makes a bundle look bulky. Too short means the cable pulls tight, lifts the tray, or drags a device off the desk when you move something.

The fix is to buy the length you need rather than the length that was in stock. Before ordering, measure the actual path: from the device, along the route, to the socket, plus a little slack for moving the desk or the monitor.

For a small desk, the common needs are short: a half metre between a monitor and a dock, a metre from the dock to the power, and a longer run only if the socket is far away.

## Trays, clips, and boxes: what each one is for

Once power and routing are decided, the accessories do the tidying.

**An under-desk tray** hides the run along the back edge. It is the single most useful piece, and it is also the one that fails most often, because a tray that is too small fills up and stops closing. Buy one with more capacity than you think you need.

**A spine or a sleeve** groups cables that travel together. Useful for a run between the desk and the floor, and awkward for anything that has to branch off.

**Adhesive clips** are for a fixed, light cable on a clean surface. They fail on textured or dusty surfaces, and they leave marks when removed. Treat them as temporary.

**A box on the desk** hides the strip and the chargers. It works, but only if the cables entering it are already grouped, otherwise it becomes a place to hide a tangle.

**A cable tray that mounts to the desk frame** is worth the effort on an adjustable desk, because the run moves with the surface instead of stretching.

## The test that tells you it worked

A tidy desk is not one that looks tidy standing still. It is one that survives being changed.

Try this after you finish:

1. Raise the desk to standing height, if it adjusts. Nothing should pull, stretch, or catch.
2. Move the monitor through its full range on its arm. The cable should follow without tugging.
3. Unplug the device you use most. You should be able to do it without opening a bundle.
4. Pull the desk away from the wall. The cables should stay attached to the desk, not to the wall.

If all four pass, the setup will stay tidy. If any fails, fix that one thing now rather than adding more clips.

## A short checklist

- Sort out power first: where the sockets are decides everything else.
- Mount the strip to the desk so an adjustable desk does not stretch cables.
- Route cables in one direction, along the back edge.
- Keep daily charging cables loose, outside any bundle.
- Measure the real path and buy the length you need, not the length in stock.
- Choose a tray with more capacity than you expect to need.
- Run the four movement tests before calling it finished.

Cable management is a layout problem with a shopping list attached. Solve the layout, and the accessories are cheap and obvious.
