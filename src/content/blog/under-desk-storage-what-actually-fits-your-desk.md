Under-desk storage looks like the simplest purchase in a home office. Choose a drawer unit, slide it under the desk, done. Then it arrives and the top drawer catches the frame, or the unit is two inches too tall for the apron, or it fits but the bottom drawer opens into the doorway.

The measurements that decide this are in the room, not the product photograph. Here are the six clearances, four storage types, and a worksheet for before you buy.

## The clearances that decide under-desk storage fit

Six numbers decide fit, and none is the desk's advertised height.

| Clearance | Measure | Why it decides fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vertical opening | Floor to the lowest point under the desk | A rear beam can sit below the top |
| Knee width | Between the legs, front and back | You and the chair share the opening |
| Pull-out depth | Unit depth plus slide travel | A full drawer needs its depth again |
| Caster height | Floor to wheel top, unit as delivered | Adds height the spec sheet may hide |
| Arm clearance | Floor to the armrest underside | The chair stops at the unit if arms are lower |
| Behind and beside | Wall to desk edge, unit to leg | Skirting and plugs push the unit forward |

OSHA's computer workstation guidance treats under-desk space as legroom first. It recommends keeping the area "free of items such as files, CPUs, books, and storage," and puts leg clearance under the desktop at 20 to 28 inches (50 to 72 cm). That is the tension. Any cabinet removes legroom from the footprint it occupies, so the useful question is which part of the opening you give up.

## Measure the opening before you shop

Do this at the height you work at, and at both settings on an adjustable desk. The lowest setting limits what can live underneath.

Measure to the underside, not the top. A desk listed at 29 1/2 inches may have less clear space once the frame and cable tray are counted. Check the front edge and the back, where a rear brace hangs lower.

Then measure the floor and the wall gap. A rug changes how a caster rolls, and skirting or a radiator pushes the unit forward until its drawers open into your knees.

## Height: desk height minus thigh clearance

Subtract the unit's height from the underside height. IKEA lists the 14 1/8 inch ALEX drawer unit at 27 1/2 inches. Under a 29 1/2 inch MICKE desk, the gap is two inches.

That is enough to slide the unit in. It is not thigh clearance. OSHA's 20 to 28 inch figure describes the height a seated person needs under the work surface, and a 27 1/2 inch cabinet removes nearly all of it where the cabinet sits.

Put the unit at one end and keep the other end clear. A 41 3/8 inch desk with a 14 1/8 inch unit leaves 27 1/4 inches of nominal width. The 26 3/8 inch ALEX on casters leaves 15 inches, which is not enough.

Adjustable desks change the arithmetic. UPLIFT lists its two-leg frame at 22.6 to 48.7 inches, a range it says fits 95 percent of users and meets ANSI/BIFMA G1-2013. A 26 inch cabinet under a desk that descends to 22.6 inches means the desk stops above it, or collision detection stops it. Measure with casters fitted, because the wheels add height. [Desk height and keyboard position](/blog/desk-height-and-keyboard-position-setting-up-for-comfort/) is the other half of this calculation.

## Width and knee space: the chair is part of the measurement

The ADA standards set a floor for accessible knee and toe space: at least 30 inches wide, 17 inches deep, and 27 inches high at the knee, with 9 inches of toe space. Those are accessibility minimums, not comfort targets, but they show how much clear width a knee zone takes.

Your chair adds to that. Measure across the armrests, then check whether chair and unit can share the opening. Armrest height decides whether the chair rolls in at all. If the armrest is lower than the unit, the chair stops. Measure from the floor to the armrest underside at its lowest setting.

The fixed ALEX used here is 14 1/8 inches wide. The 26 3/8 inch caster model is nearly twice that. The narrow one leaves more knee space and stores less.

## Depth: pull-out travel and space behind the drawer

Depth is the clearance people forget, because a drawer unit can be deeper than the desk.

IKEA lists the fixed ALEX at 22 7/8 inches deep and the MICKE desk at 19 5/8 inches. The unit is 3 1/4 inches deeper than the desk. Push it to the wall and it sits proud of the front edge. The 26 3/8 inch caster version is 18 7/8 inches deep, leaving 3/4 inch under the same desk.

Add the drawer travel. Accuride describes its 3832E slide as full extension, in lengths from 6 to 28 inches, rated to 100 lb with a 24 inch maximum drawer width and 0.50 inch of side space. A 20 inch drawer on a full-extension slide needs about 20 inches of clear space in front of the cabinet.

Inside depth is smaller again: 20 1/2 inches on the 22 7/8 inch ALEX and 16 1/2 inches on the 18 7/8 inch caster version. Check the inside figure for paper or a laptop. The same measurements decide [whether a filing cabinet fits under a desk](/blog/how-to-choose-a-filing-cabinet-that-fits-under-a-desk/).

## Four types of under-desk storage

| Type | Legroom effect | Cable routing | What it stores |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drawer pedestal | Blocks the most knee and foot space | Blocks the rear cable drop | The most, in one place |
| Rolling cabinet | Moveable, but taller | Can trap a cable under a wheel | Most, but check the rated load |
| Clamp-on tray | Takes knee depth, not floor space | Usually clear below | Shallow items |
| Under-desk shelf | Low, but takes foot space | Easiest to route around | A printer, bag, or box |

A **drawer pedestal** stores the most and blocks the most. A hung pedestal keeps the floor clear but takes knee depth. A **rolling cabinet** moves out of the way but is taller. Check the rated load: IKEA lists 40 lb for the 26 3/8 inch ALEX on casters and 26 lb for the fixed 14 1/8 inch unit. A **clamp-on tray** needs a clear clamping surface and knee depth. An **under-desk shelf** collects dust and cables.

## Cable routing around storage

Storage changes the cable path. A pedestal against the wall blocks the drop for monitor and power cables. A rolling cabinet can trap a cable under a wheel. A shelf holds a power strip in the dust behind the desk.

Plan the route before the storage position. On an adjustable desk, cables must move with the desk, so anything fixed to the floor is a snag point. IKEA's MICKE hides cables at the back, exactly where a tall unit blocks them. Keep cables out of the drawer travel, and fix them to the wall or frame. The [cable management plan for a small desk](/blog/cable-management-for-a-small-desk/) settles power and lengths first.

## A measurement worksheet you can copy

Write everything in the same unit, and convert the room measurement once.

| What to measure | How | Pass condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Underside height | Tape, front and back | Above unit height plus a margin |
| Clear width | Tape, front and rear brace | Room for unit and chair |
| Desk depth | Tape, edge to wall | Unit depth plus travel |
| Wall gap | Tape at the floor | Sits flat, no fouling |
| Unit height as delivered | Spec sheet, then tape | Below the underside height |
| Drawer travel | Spec sheet | Clear space in front |
| Slide load rating | Spec sheet | Above the loaded drawer weight |
| Armrest height | Tape, lowest setting | Above the unit where the chair rolls |

If a row fails, change the unit or the layout, not the desk's stopping point.

## Desk heights against storage heights

The table uses sourced heights and subtraction. The middle column is arithmetic, not advice.

| Desk surface height (source) | Tallest storage leaving 2 inches | ALEX fixed, 27 1/2 inches | ALEX on casters, 26 inches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 22.6 inches (UPLIFT frame, lowest) | 20.6 inches | Does not fit | Does not fit |
| 29.5 inches (IKEA MICKE) | 27.5 inches | Fits, 2 inches to spare | Fits, 3.5 inches to spare |
| 48.7 inches (UPLIFT frame, highest) | 46.7 inches | Fits, 21.2 inches to spare | Fits, 22.7 inches to spare |

The 29.5 inch row is one example, not a standard. Measure your own desk: the underside is lower than the surface wherever there is an apron, a frame beam, or the desk's own drawer. A unit that fits at standing height can be too tall at seated height.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much space does a drawer unit need?

Measure the underside height, then subtract the unit's overall height. Leave a visible margin rather than a zero-gap fit, because the desk may have a rear beam and the floor may not be flat. Keep one side clear for your knees.

### Can I put a drawer unit under a standing desk?

Only if the unit is shorter than the desk's lowest height. UPLIFT lists its frame from 22.6 to 48.7 inches, so a 26 inch cabinet cannot stay underneath at the lowest setting. Move the storage before lowering, or accept a higher minimum.

### Do casters make a drawer unit taller?

Yes. The wheels add height, so the overall height with casters is taller than the body. Use the overall figure from the spec sheet and confirm it with a tape measure.

### How deep does a drawer need to be to open fully?

It needs its own depth again in front of the cabinet if the slides are full extension. Accuride lists its 3832E slide from 6 to 28 inches, so check the slide length and the clear floor in front.

### Is a clamp-on tray better than a pedestal?

It is better when floor space is tight and you only need shallow storage. It is worse for files or a laptop, because it takes knee depth and holds less. Check the clamp surface and desk frame first.
