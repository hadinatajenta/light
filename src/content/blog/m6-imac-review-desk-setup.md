The iMac with M6 arrived on October 27, 2026, two years after the M4 overhaul and one chip generation late. Pre-orders opened October 28 and the first units landed on November 6, per [Apple's newsroom announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-unveils-new-imac-with-m6/). The chassis is the same 24-inch design from 2021, the seven colors return, and as [The Verge notes](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1018467/apple-imac-m6-chip-announced-price-release), the design otherwise appears largely unchanged. What changes is the silicon, the storage tier options, and a long list of small checkout decisions that quietly move the final price by hundreds of dollars.

This review reads the M6 iMac the way a desk setup does: what it benchmarks, what it plugs into, what the storage tier means for a real library, and above all whether the stand can get the screen to your eye line. The performance numbers here come from [TechRadar's five days with the M6 iMac](https://www.techradar.com/computing/macs/ive-tested-apples-new-m6-imac-and-its-still-the-best-all-in-one-desktop-you-can-buy), [Applesfera's lab tests](https://www.applesfera.com/analisis/imac-m6-analisis-caracteristicas-precio-especificaciones), and Apple's own claims, with every figure attributed to its source.

## Quick answer

- The M6 chip earns its bump: about 4,553 single-core and 20,038 multi-core in Geekbench 6, roughly 25 percent and 36 percent above the M4 iMac in TechRadar's testing.
- Storage speed is no longer the story; capacity is. The 256GB base tier is the bottleneck, and the checkout page is where you fix it.
- Base model: $1,499 with 10 cores and two Thunderbolt 4 ports. $1,699 buys 12 cores, four ports, 2.5Gb Ethernet, and support for a second 6K display.
- The stand still only tilts, roughly 5 degrees forward and 25 degrees back, so screen height is a riser, an arm, or a compromise.
- A Mac mini M6 costs $899 and is faster on paper at the base tier. The iMac's case is the 4.5K panel, the camera, and the speakers you do not buy separately.

## Performance: the M6 earns its version number

Apple skipped the M5 on the 24-inch iMac and went straight to the M6, the same 2nm chip that landed in the Mac mini this fall. The base chip gained two CPU cores and two GPU cores over the M4, now with 10 cores in each, and every GPU core carries a Neural Accelerator alongside a dual 16-core Neural Engine.

The numbers TechRadar recorded in its Geekbench 6 testing are the cleanest summary:

| Benchmark | Result | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geekbench 6 single-core | about 4,553 | TechRadar M6 iMac testing |
| Geekbench 6 multi-core | about 20,038 | TechRadar M6 iMac testing |
| SSD read | about 6,525 MB/s | Applesfera, Blackmagic Disk Speed Test |
| SSD write | about 5,356 MB/s | Applesfera, Blackmagic Disk Speed Test |
| Internal SSD speed | up to 2x faster than M4 | Apple newsroom announcement |
| On-device AI performance | up to 5x faster than M4 | Apple newsroom announcement |
| Overall performance | up to 18x faster than the most popular Intel iMac | Apple newsroom announcement |

TechRadar's comparison with the M4 iMac lands at roughly 25 percent higher single-core and 36 percent higher multi-core, which is a real generation of speed, not a marketing rounding. The Intel figure matters for a specific buyer: an office still running an Intel-era iMac, the machine Apple claims the M6 surpasses by up to 18x. Apple's "up to" language applies to its configured comparisons, but nothing in the independent numbers contradicts the direction.

For a desk workload the useful summary is even simpler. Across five days as a daily driver, TechRadar found email, browsing, message replies, Keynote presentations, scrolling Photos, and FaceTime calls all ran without lag, and the 4,480 x 2,520 resolution scaled cleanly for text-heavy pages. That is the honest bar for an all-in-one: the M6 clears it with speed to spare, and SSD wait times, at about 6,525 MB/s reads, never become the story you tell.

Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6, and the first N1 networking chip in an iMac round out the internal story, with 2.5Gb Ethernet among the options depending on tier. For spreadsheets, photo libraries, and browser-heavy workdays, you will never wait on this machine.

## The 24-inch 4.5K panel, and why it cannot live anywhere else

The panel is unchanged: 24 inches at 4,480 x 2,520, 500 nits of brightness, P3 wide color with True Tone, and the nano-texture glass option reserved for the 12-core model. That resolution at this diagonal lands the screen near the distance sweet spot: on a desk about 60 cm deep, the panel sits close to the recommended viewing distance, which is exactly where 27-inch panels start to crowd the depth. The fit math is covered in our [24 vs 27 inch monitor guide for a 60 cm shallow desk](/blog/24-vs-27-inch-monitor-at-60-cm-on-a-shallow-desk/).

Two hard limits decide whether this box fits your office. First, Apple silicon iMacs have no target display mode, and the internal 4.5K panel cannot be reused elsewhere. The screen travels with the computer and dies with it, so the iMac is a package deal, not a display with a computer bolted on. Second, the panel and its angle belong to the stand in the next section, because the camera quality, the audio, and the eye position are all points of the same geometry.

The built parts around the screen are part of the pitch, though. A 12MP Center Stage camera with Desk View, a six-speaker system, and a three-microphone array come inside the same chassis, per Apple's announcement. For a small home office that would otherwise spend on a webcam and desktop speakers, that bundle is real money the spec sheet hides.

## The 256GB storage decision

Storage is the decision the checkout page makes for you. The SSD itself is fast by any external standard: Applesfera's Blackmagic Disk Speed Test measured read speeds of about 6,525 MB/s and write speeds of 5,356 MB/s, so wait times at the drive level are a non-issue even at entry capacity. The problem is volume. System, apps, and a 200GB photo library consume most of 256GB before a video project folder asks for room, and iCloud offloading makes the constraint look abstract right up until you travel offline.

The upgrade ladder is short. Both models allow 512GB and 1TB. The 12-core four-port model raises the ceiling to 2TB and the memory ceiling to 32GB, where the two-port model stops at 24GB, as [MacObserver's pricing summary](https://www.macobserver.com/news/imac-m6-price/) and [PetaPixel's review](https://petapixel.com/2026/11/05/imac-with-m6-review-the-best-all-in-one-computer-for-photographers/) both document. Since storage and memory are not user-serviceable after dispatch, the honest recommendation is blunt: if the library is already 200GB and growing, configure 512GB or 1TB at checkout rather than promising to clean up later.

## Two models, one port split, and the checkout fees

The iMac divides into two builds, and most of what matters is inside the first price jump.

- $1,499: 10-core CPU and 10-core GPU, two Thunderbolt 4 ports, one external display at up to 6K at 60Hz or 4K at 165Hz, Magic Keyboard and Magic Mouse included, 2.5Gb Ethernet available as a $30 option.
- $1,699: 12-core CPU and 12-core GPU, four Thunderbolt 4 ports, support for up to two external 6K displays, 2.5Gb Ethernet included, Touch ID keyboard included, and the nano-texture glass option available on this tier only.

The external display step is the deep divide. At $1,499 the answer to "can I add a second screen" is one panel, at 6K at 60Hz or 4K at 165Hz. At $1,699 it is two 6K displays, which is the configuration Apple's announcement describes at more than 50 million pixels of workspace. Anyone planning a two-monitor desk should treat the $1,699 tier as the true entry price.

The small fees add up. Swapping the included Magic Mouse for the Magic Trackpad costs $50, and on the base model 2.5Gb Ethernet is a $30 option rather than a default, per The Verge's review of the checkout page. The Magic Keyboard ships standard without Touch ID; the Touch ID version is a paid upgrade on the $1,499 model and included with the 12-core. The Ethernet port itself lives on the power brick, not the computer, which keeps the desk cable count at one. Education pricing drops both models to $1,449 and $1,599. U.S. shoppers on Apple Card can split the price at 0 percent APR, Apple Trade In applies credit for an older computer, and MacObserver lists the Apple Upgrade lease at $28.99 per month over 36 months for the $1,499 build, all documented in [Apple's announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-unveils-new-imac-with-m6/) and [CNET's review](https://www.cnet.com/tech/computing/apple-imac-m6-2026-review/).

## The stand still only tilts, so measure before you order

The included stand only tilts, roughly 5 degrees forward and 25 degrees back, and it does not adjust height. Apple moved ergonomics off the hinge years ago, and the M6 keeps that position. Your only height controls are a riser underneath the footprint or the no-cost built-in VESA mount adapter configuration paired with an arm. If the desk is shallow, the riser route is usually safer for reach and depth, and our [monitor riser vs arm comparison](/blog/monitor-riser-vs-arm-when-a-shelf-beats-an-arm/) covers exactly when a shelf beats an arm. If an arm is going under the chin of this 24-inch panel, order the iMac in its VESA configuration rather than swapping stands later, and check the plate specifics in our [VESA mount compatibility guide](/blog/vesa-mount-compatibility-plate-sizes-and-screw-patterns/).

The chassis measurements matter for the space decision. With the stand the whole thing is 54.7 x 46.1 x 14.7 cm and 4.42 or 4.44 kg depending on port count, so the depth your desk actually gives up is 14.7 cm, and the height your eyes live at is whatever the fixed tilt happens to land.

The rule for screen height holds regardless of which route you take: the top of the glass should land at or slightly below your seated eye line, and at a standard fixed desk that rarely happens out of the box, especially for taller users. So measure your seated eye height and desk depth before ordering, and pair the measurement with the break-and-lighting habits in our [eye strain guide](/blog/reducing-eye-strain-at-a-desk-distance-lighting-breaks/). A riser takes 10 minutes and roughly the cost of the Ethernet option to get right. An arm gets there faster, sets viewing distance as well as height, and our [monitor height and eye line guide](/blog/monitor-height-on-an-arm-eye-line-tilt-and-viewing-distance/) covers the placement it enables.

## Mac mini M6: the $600 question

| Feature | iMac M6 ($1,499) | Mac mini M6 ($899) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPU / GPU | 10 cores each | 12 cores each |
| Memory / storage | 16GB / 256GB | 16GB / 256GB |
| Display, camera, audio | 24-inch 4.5K, 12MP camera, six-speaker system | none |
| Keyboard / mouse | Magic Keyboard and Magic Mouse included | none |
| Ports | 2 Thunderbolt 4 on the back | 2 USB-C and headphone jack in front, 3 Thunderbolt 4, HDMI, and 2.5Gb Ethernet behind |
| External displays | one 6K at 60Hz or 4K at 165Hz | up to three via Thunderbolt and HDMI |

The mini undercuts the iMac by $600 with two more cores of each type and no peripherals to speak of. The memory bandwidth of the 16GB mini config is 153GB/s, and 170GB/s at 24GB or 32GB, per [Apple's Mac mini support specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-us/128108). The mini line also steps up to the M5 Pro at $1,699 for buyers who want Thunderbolt 5 and 307GB/s. Once a 4.5K-or-better display, a webcam, speakers, and Apple's keyboard and mouse are priced onto the $899 base, the iMac and the accessorized mini converge; Tom's Guide put a setup comparable to the iMac at $1,350 to $1,450 before tax. The mini wins when you already own a good monitor; the iMac wins when you are starting a desk from empty. ## Verdict

The M6 iMac is the same 24-inch all-in-one it has been since 2021, and this year that fact stops being a complaint only because the M6 finally moves the speed line: about 4,553 and 20,038 in Geekbench 6, roughly 25 and 36 percent over M4, with the 2x storage claim and 5x AI claim behind it. For an office still stuck on an Intel-era iMac, the up to 18x figure is the entire argument.

Buy the $1,499 model as the default, and pay the $1,699 tier only for one of three reasons: two 6K displays, 2.5Gb Ethernet and Touch ID included, or the nano-texture glass and the 32GB ceiling. Price the storage tier honestly against your libraries, since 256GB is a promise to organize files that most households do not keep. And make the stand a measured plan before checkout: it only tilts, roughly 5 degrees forward and 25 degrees back, so decide now between a riser and the free VESA version with an arm, put the top of the panel at or just below your eye line, and treat the rest of the desk as furniture you keep.
