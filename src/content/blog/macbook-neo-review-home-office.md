At $699 the MacBook Neo is again the cheapest Mac laptop Apple sells, and it remains the strangest MacBook in years. Apple built it in March 2026 on the A18 Pro, the chip from the iPhone 16 Pro, fixed the memory at 8GB with no upgrade path, left the keyboard backlight and Touch ID out of the base model, and staked the budget line on the result. The launch price of $599 lasted until June 25, when Apple lifted it by $100 during the memory shortage. Eight months of independent testing later, this review collects the measurements and asks the desk buyer's question: can a machine this cheap hold down a desk?

## Quick answer

- For browsing, documents, and calls the Neo is fast. Ars measured Geekbench 6 single-core runs roughly 40 to 42 percent faster than the M1 MacBook Air.
- Multi-core is the trade: roughly a tie with the M1, and about 70 percent of the M1 Air's throughput in multi-core-heavy work such as video encoding.
- The 8GB ceiling is less of a wall than the arguments assumed. Macworld spent a week pushing the Neo through Premiere Pro edits and 59 open Chrome tabs without a single stall.
- Charging caps at about 30W no matter how big a brick you plug in. On a 140W charger The Verge counted 45 to 50 minutes to 50 percent and about two hours to a full charge.
- Desk duties come down to one port: a single external display up to 4K at 60Hz over the 10Gbps USB-C socket, with USB 2 as the second.
- Take the $799 512GB model if the budget allows; it is the only configuration with Touch ID, and Apple's education discount holds the base model at $599 for students.

## What $699 and $799 buy in November

The Neo went on sale March 11, 2026, in one size: a 13-inch aluminum body at 2.7 pounds and 12.7mm thick, in silver, blush, citrus, or indigo, with the colors carrying into color-matched keycaps. The 256GB model launched at $599 and the 512GB model, which adds a Touch ID sensor in the power button, launched at $699. On June 25, 2026, Apple raised prices across the Mac line during the memory shortage, and the Neo moved to $699 and $799, where both configs sit today; [MacRumors documented the price increase](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/25/apple-just-raised-macbook-neo-prices/) the day it happened.

Students have it better than the June headlines suggested. Apple's education discount runs $100 below list across the line, which puts the base Neo at $599 and the 512GB model at $699 on the education store. The exact figure has shuffled around in the months after the repricing, so check Apple's education store before ordering rather than trusting a screenshot.

Nothing else is configurable. Memory is 8GB on every model, SSD choices end at 512GB, and the four finishes change nothing under the hood. The panel is a 13-inch Liquid Retina LCD at 2,408 by 1,506, 500 nits, and sRGB color only.

## Benchmarks: fast where buyers live, capped where they don't

[Ars Technica's review](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/03/apple-macbook-neo-review-can-a-mac-get-by-with-an-iphones-processor-inside/) carries the measurements most worth trusting here, because the reviewers ran the Neo against their own long-term reference, the 2020 M1 MacBook Air. Single-core, the mode in which almost everything a desk worker actually does runs, measured roughly 40 to 42 percent faster than the M1. Multi-core measured a near dead heat, with the M1's four performance cores outmuscling the A18 Pro's two for a lead of a couple of percentage points.

Multi-core-heavy loads are where the missing cores bill. Across multi-core benchmarks, video encoding, and GPU-tied tests, Ars measured the Neo completing roughly 70 percent of what the M1 MacBook Air gets through in the same work. The strangest measurement is also the most useful: Cinebench 2024's GPU tests refuse to run on the Neo at all, because the memory allocation those tests demand does not exist inside 8GB.

The chassis has no fan backing any of this up, so nothing spills out warm except silence. Airflow is among the cheapest things a desk can fix, and [our guide to laptop cooling and when a stand is enough](/blog/laptop-cooling-when-a-stand-is-enough/) walks through what a stand and a taper help with here.

## The 8GB question, answered with a week of abuse

Macworld's Roman Loyola turned the "8GB is not enough" argument into an experiment. Over a week he used the Neo the way reviewers tell you not to: he edited the 67-minute episode 974 of the Macworld Podcast in Adobe Premiere Pro, with trims, lower thirds, a multi-camera sequence, and audio cleanup; he edited 20 minutes of 4K footage shot at 60 frames per second on an iPhone 17 Pro Max; he then ran four-hour browser sessions without closing a single tab. [His stress-test log, titled I pushed my MacBook Neo to the limit. It didn't break](https://www.macworld.com/article/3090343/macbook-neo-stress-test-adaobe-premiere-safari-google-chrome-tabs.html), tracks the whole week.

The numbers: during the podcast edit, Activity Monitor recorded 2.58GB in swap, and the full 1080p export of that episode took 31 minutes, about 10 minutes longer than an M5 Max MacBook Pro needed for the same file. The 4K edit finished at 1.75GB of swap. Browsers were the extreme case: 41 Safari tabs pushed swap past 4GB, the same Chrome level crossed 5GB, and when he forced it to 59 open tabs, swap grew to nearly 8GB, equal to the installed memory. The Neo kept switching tabs easily the whole time. Across the week he reports no stutter, no beachballs, and no stalls.

None of this makes 8GB spacious; it makes it livable for the work that reaches a $699 laptop. The wall sits in the tier above, where The Verge found a 60-tab Chrome pile-up with Slack, Signal, Steam, and Music running behind it slowing to a crawl, and Lightroom Classic's Develop module throwing beachballs on RAW processing.

## Battery and charging: a 30W ceiling

Apple rates the 36.5Wh battery at up to 16 hours of video and 11 hours of wireless web. In [Macworld's roundup testing](https://www.macworld.com/article/667144/best-macbook-air-vs-pro-neo-compared.html), a fixed-brightness video loop at 150 nits ran 13 hours and 15 minutes. On a real workday built around a 40-minute video call and music streaming, [The Verge's reviewer](https://www.theverge.com/tech/891741/apple-macbook-neo-a18-pro-review) logged a little under nine hours with the screen at 75 percent brightness. Both numbers beat your eight-hour workday with room to spare, which is the battery promise that actually matters at a desk.

The charging story is stranger. The bundled charger is a 20W USB-C block, and in the UK and EU the Neo ships with no charger at all. On that stock brick, ChargerLAB measured a peak of just 18W, per [MacRumors' charging test](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/03/20/macbook-neo-charging-test/). The ceiling sits on the laptop's side of the cable: pointed at a 140W MacBook Pro adapter, The Verge found the Neo drawing only about 30W, going from 0 to about 50 percent in 45 to 50 minutes and taking roughly two hours to a full charge, so a bigger brick buys nothing beyond a certain point. [ChargerLAB's compatibility sweep, covered by iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/100281/macbook-neo-charging-test-confirms-30w-limit-across-all-chargers-video), backs the number: every adapter rated 30W or higher, Apple's or third-party, delivered between 29 and 31W. The Japanese site [AAPL Ch.](https://applech2.com/archives/20260320-macbook-neo-support-30w-charger.html) added the footnote that the laptop requests up to 45W in System Information while never actually negotiating above 30W in practice.

For a desk, this shrugs itself out. Leave the bundled 20W block plugged in behind the monitor, stop thinking about it, and do not buy wattage: a 45W-or-higher USB-C charger delivers the same speed as the brick you already own.

## Ports: two sockets, one of them slow

Both USB-C ports sit on the left side, unlabeled, which The Verge summed up in three words: no labels, no sense made. The 10Gbps USB 3 socket is the one nearer the hinge; the front socket is USB 2 at 480Mbps; the headphone jack sits alone on the right edge. The rear port is the whole display story, with DisplayPort 1.4 support for [one external display up to 4K at 60Hz, per Apple's spec page](https://www.apple.com/macbook-neo/specs/), and there is a trap: plug the monitor into the front socket and macOS warns you that you used the wrong one. Macworld [published a port guide for the Neo](https://www.macworld.com/article/3083689/macbook-neo-ports-which-to-use.html) that makes the distinctions plain.

That single socket is still enough to drive a good 27-inch 4K panel, which covers most of what a home office needs. Picking the panel is a separate decision, and [our home-office monitor guide](/blog/how-to-choose-a-monitor-for-a-home-office/) covers resolution, stand, and ports before you spend.

There is no MagSafe anywhere. Either socket charges at full power, so the cost of that omission is committing half the machine's port capacity to the charging cable for the life of the laptop. A dock does not fix the one-display limit, but it still earns its place by turning three devices into one cable; [our USB-C dock guide](/blog/how-to-choose-a-usb-c-docking-station-for-a-laptop/) covers what to look for at this speed class. Pay for nothing more, because [Thunderbolt 5 docks](/blog/thunderbolt-5-docks-explained-bandwidth-boost-dual-4k-pcie-storage/) are priced for bandwidth this laptop cannot speak.

One dock, one brick, and one 1.5m lead also make the Neo easy to wire once and forget; the hardware that keeps a desk that tidy lives in [our cable management for a small desk](/blog/cable-management-for-a-small-desk/) guide.

## Keyboard, trackpad, and panel

The panel is bright and vivid; The Verge found it comfortable outdoors in everything short of direct glare. Its ceiling is color, not brightness: sRGB only, against the Air's P3 wide color and True Tone, so gamut-critical photo work stops here.

The keyboard types like every current MacBook, symbols on the modifier keys and all, and the base model's missing backlight is felt nightly. The light-colored, color-matched keycaps on most finishes help a little in dim rooms, but the honest fix is a desk lamp. The 1080p webcam is sharp and clear in dim light but lacks Center Stage framing; if a daily video habit outgrows it, [our webcam guide for a small home office](/blog/how-to-choose-a-webcam-for-a-small-home-office/) lists what to add and what to skip. The dual side-firing speakers fill a small room and thin out at volume.

The trackpad is the pleasant surprise: mechanical rather than haptic, clicking anywhere on the surface so fingers never hunt for a sweet spot. The Verge graded it an A against a C for the keyboard, and in its report card it was the Neo's strongest component.

The MacBook Air's $600 markup buys what the Neo cannot reach at any configuration. [AppleInsider's testing](https://appleinsider.com/inside/macbook-air/vs/macbook-neo-vs-m5-macbook-air-budget-notebooks-compared) put the M5 Air above 17,000 in Geekbench 6 multi-core while the Neo managed a little over 8,500, and two Thunderbolt 4 ports, MagSafe, P3 color, a second external display, and four to five extra rated hours sit on top. The premium reads as the price of a machine that still feels standard in five years.

The Mac mini M6, announced in August and on sale from September 22, needs its own monitor and keyboard, yet at $899 it outclasses the Neo on every number in that table: a 12-core M6, up to 170GB/s of bandwidth at higher memory tiers, and three supported displays against one. As a fixed desk machine it pairs naturally with the same monitor a Neo would use. ## A second generation is already on the schedule

Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, whose reporting [MacRumors passed along in late October](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/27/macbook-neo-2-first-half-2027-report/), expects a second-generation Neo in the first half of 2027 with an A19 Pro chip, 12GB of RAM, and new colors. The memory figure is the meaningful word, since 12GB would raise this generation's hardest limit by half. Gurman's timing has concrete sourcing behind it, but Apple has announced nothing, so treat it as a planning input rather than a promise. A buyer keeping the machine five years should weigh the timeline, and a buyer replacing a dying laptop today should not.

## Who this laptop is for

Three buyers fit the Neo almost perfectly: a student whose workload is browsers, documents, and video calls; a household that wants a third machine no one will be precious about; a first-time Mac buyer for whom $1,299 was never going to happen. The upgrade worth considering is the same for all three: the $799 step-up that doubles storage and adds Touch ID, the sensor you touch a hundred times a day. Storage pulls double duty as the pressure valve, because macOS leans on the SSD for swap when memory runs tight, and a larger drive leaves room for both.

## Verdict

The MacBook Neo is the most honest cheap laptop Apple has ever built, and its value survived the June price increase. Ars's measurements say it runs single-core work faster than the M1 ever did, and pays for that speed in throughput. Macworld's stress week says the 8GB wall sits farther out than the pre-launch arguments assumed, with no stutter while Premiere and 59 tabs ran. The Verge's charging data says the wall socket is a shrug rather than an obstacle. What a desk cannot fix is short: sRGB-only color, no keyboard backlight, one display, and a 480Mbps second port where the Air carries Thunderbolt. Everything a desk can fix, one monitor, one dock, one cable, the Neo handles better than its price class says it should. Buy it for the person whose workload matches the spec sheet, take the 512GB model if the budget survives it, and let a MacBook Air or an M6 mini take over when multi-core becomes the job.
