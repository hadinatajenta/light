A Herman Miller Aeron vs Steelcase Leap V2 comparison usually turns into an argument about brands. It should be about fit. The Aeron is a mesh chair in three fixed sizes. The Leap is a foam chair in one size with a sliding seat pan. Both solve the same fit problem from opposite directions.

## Quick answer

- Aeron seat depth is fixed by size: 16 inches in A, 17 in B, 18.5 in C. It cannot be changed later.
- The Leap has a 19.25-inch seat width and a pan that slides about 3 inches, with an overall depth of 21.75 to 24.75 inches.
- Aeron weight ratings are 300, 350, and 400 pounds by size. The Leap is rated to 400, the Leap Plus to 500.
- Both carry a 12-year parts and labor warranty and are configured to order, so price follows the build.
- Lumbar is a purchase decision on the Aeron and standard on the Leap.

## Mesh versus foam: what you are sitting on

Herman Miller lists the Aeron seat and back as suspension. 8Z Pellicle is eight zones of elastomeric suspension with no foam layer, so you sit on tension and the seat feels firm. The Leap is foam under fabric, leather, or vinyl, with "flexible edges that offer a bit of a bend" at the front and sides of the seat.

Mesh does not compress, take a body impression, or trap heat. Foam spreads load over a softer surface but wears first and runs warmer. Run hot or sit still for hours and mesh has the advantage. Want a cushioned sit and the Leap is the familiar choice. The [mesh versus foam comparison](/blog/mesh-vs-foam-office-chair-seats-what-to-check-for-long-sitting/) covers wear in more detail.

## Seat height and seat depth

Figures come from Herman Miller's Aeron spec page and Steelcase's Leap page and spec guide.

| Measurement | Aeron A | Aeron B | Aeron C | Leap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seat height | 14.4 to 19.3 in | 14.8 to 22.8 in | 15.8 to 22.8 in | 15.5 to 20.5 in |
| Seat depth | 16 in, fixed | 17 in, fixed | 18.5 in, fixed | adjustable, 3 in travel |
| Overall depth | 27 to 27.5 in | 27.5 to 28.3 in | 27.5 to 28.3 in | 21.75 to 24.75 in |
| Seat width | not published | not published | not published | 19.25 in |

The Aeron's depth belongs to the chair, not to a setting. Size A is 16 inches and size C is 18.5. A B that is too short for long thighs stays too short, so the [guide to seat depth and height](/blog/chair-seat-depth-and-height-office-chair-sizing/) is worth reading before ordering.

The Leap's pan moves. Steelcase gives overall depth as 21.75 to 24.75 inches and labels that range adjustable seat depth, so the pan travels about 3 inches, enough for a tall user or two people sharing one chair. Steelcase publishes no cushion depth figure, so 3 inches is all you can work from.

Seat height separates them at the bottom: the Aeron A starts at 14.4 inches, the Leap at 15.5. If a 15.5-inch seat leaves your feet dangling, only the Aeron A goes lower. At the top, the Aeron B and C reach 22.8 inches while the Leap stops at 20.5; a 7-inch cylinder raises the Leap to 17 to 24 inches. Herman Miller's fit chart assumes the extended-height cylinder.

## Seat width and the three Aeron sizes

Herman Miller publishes overall width, not cushion width: 27.5 inches for A, 28.3 for B and C, up to 29.3 and 31.6 with fully adjustable arms. Steelcase publishes a 19.25-inch seat width and 15 to 20 inches between the arms, the more useful pair for hip clearance.

The sizes change more than width: the fit reference uses height and weight and recommends B for anyone in the A/B or B/C overlap.

## Lumbar support and back design

The Leap's lower back support is included and adjustable. Every Leap has height-adjustable lumbar, moved by sliding the side handles, plus a firmness dial. The spec guide puts lumbar height at 5.25 to 10.25 inches.

The Aeron's support is chosen at purchase. Herman Miller offers PostureFit, adjustable lumbar, or none, and PostureFit SL is a sacral pad with a secondary lumbar pad. The $2,045 store build is a size C with Basic Back Support, meaning neither.

LiveBack flexes with the recline and has a manual tension control. The Aeron's back conforms by stretching tensioned Pellicle rather than hinging.

## Arms, tilt, and headrest

| Adjustment | Aeron | Leap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arm height | 7.5 to 11.5 in fully adjustable, 8.5 in fixed | arm to floor 22 to 31 in |
| Arm directions | height and pivot, plus a separate pad depth option | height, width, depth, pivot |
| Tilt | Harmonic 2 Tilt; standard, limiter, or limiter with seat angle | four recline stops plus upright lock |
| Headrest | not offered by Herman Miller | separate model |

Steelcase's 4D arms adjust in height, width, depth, and pivot, with an arm-to-floor range of 22 to 31 inches. Herman Miller offers armless, fixed arms, height-adjustable arms with pivot, and fully adjustable arms, plus an arm pad depth option.

The Leap publishes tilt detail too: four recline stops, an upright lock, a seat pan angle of minus 1 to 3 degrees, and a seat-to-back angle of 96 to 120 degrees. Herman Miller sells standard tilt, a limiter, or a limiter with seat angle, and publishes no angles.

Of the two, only Steelcase offers a headrest: a Leap listed at $2,879.

## Weight ratings, warranty, and price

| Item | Aeron | Leap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight rating | 300 lb (A), 350 lb (B), 400 lb (C) | 400 lb; Leap Plus 500 lb |
| Warranty | 12 years, parts and labor, 3 shifts, 24-hour coverage | 12 years, multi-shift, 24/7 parts and labor |
| Store price | $2,045 to $2,730 | $2,167 |
| List price | not published | $2,506 standard, $2,879 with headrest |

Herman Miller says size A is "tested and warrantied for individuals weighing up to 300 pounds", B up to 350, C up to 400. Steelcase says the Leap is "durable enough to handle weight up to 400 lbs". A 380-pound buyer can use a Leap or an Aeron C, not an A or B.

Herman Miller covers 12 years, parts and labor, three shifts and 24-hour use, with service costs covered in the U.S. and Canada. Steelcase calls its 12-year, multi-shift, 24/7 parts and labor coverage a Limited Lifetime warranty.

Prices move with configuration. As of 27 October 2026, U.S. pricing on the Herman Miller store ran from $2,045 for a size C with basic back support to $2,730 for a loaded size B; a B with PostureFit SL, tilt limiter and seat angle, and fully adjustable arms was $2,305. The Steelcase Store listed the standard Leap at $2,167, while Steelcase's page shows no visible price at all.

Steelcase lists BIFMA, BIFMA LEVEL 3, CAL 117, EN compliance, and SCS Indoor Advantage Gold; Herman Miller lists BIFMA compliance and level certification without a level number. The [guide to reading chair certifications](/blog/how-to-read-ergonomic-chair-certifications-bifma-en-1335-greenguard/) explains which ones carry weight. Herman Miller charges $99 return shipping on performance seating plus 10 percent restocking if packaging is gone, and issues must be reported within 30 days; Steelcase's store FAQ cites a 30-day window.

## Fit check: who each chair fits

Short users should start with the Aeron A. A 14.4-inch seat height floor and 16-inch depth are the smallest pair here, and the Leap's floor is more than an inch higher. The cost is a 300-pound rating and the narrowest footprint.

Tall users have two routes. The Aeron C gives 18.5 inches of depth and 22.8 inches of height with no extra options. The Leap needs the 7-inch cylinder to reach 24 inches, and its pan extends to 24.75 inches of overall depth. A long femur favors the Leap's pan; a long torso favors the Aeron C's taller back.

People who sit six hours straight should choose seat material first, because it cannot be changed. The Aeron's mesh does not compress and moves air; the Leap's foam is softer at the start and will take an impression eventually.

Two people sharing one chair will have an easier time with the Leap: one size, a sliding pan, and 4D arms cover a range of bodies.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Steelcase Leap V2 the same chair as the Leap?

Steelcase's product page calls it Leap. The V2 label is how retailers refer to the current generation, and the specifications here come from Steelcase's current page and spec guide.

### Does the Aeron come in different sizes?

Yes. The three sizes change seat depth, seat height range, back height, overall width, and weight rating. Seat depth is 16, 17, and 18.5 inches; ratings are 300, 350, and 400 pounds.

### Which chair has better lumbar support?

The Leap includes more as standard: height-adjustable lumbar plus a firmness dial, with a published range of 5.25 to 10.25 inches. The Aeron can match it with PostureFit SL or adjustable lumbar, but only if your build includes it.

### Can you add a headrest to an Aeron?

Herman Miller lists no headrest option for the Aeron, and options are fixed at purchase. Steelcase sells a Leap with a headrest as a separate model, listed at $2,879.

### Does the Aeron or the Leap have a higher weight limit?

It depends on the Aeron size. A is rated to 300 pounds, B to 350, C to 400, matching the Leap's 400; the Leap Plus is rated to 500. Above 350 pounds, the Aeron requires size C.
