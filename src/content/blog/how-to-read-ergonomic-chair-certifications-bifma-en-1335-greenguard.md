## Quick answer

- EN 1335 is the European office chair standard: part 1 covers dimensions, part 2 safety and durability, part 3 mechanical test methods.
- EN 1335-2:2018 is based on 8 hours of daily use by users up to 110 kg.
- ANSI/BIFMA X5.1 is a US durability test battery, not a size table; BIFMA G1 is an ergonomics guideline written as fit criteria without prescribed numbers.
- GREENGUARD is a chemical-emissions certification, not an ergonomics or durability credential.
- Ask for the exact standard, part, tier, and test report sections.

An ergonomic certification sounds like one pass or fail mark. It is usually a stack of documents with different jobs. One document may test whether a chair survives repeated load, another may set how seat height is measured, and a third may measure volatile organic compounds (VOCs, chemicals that evaporate from materials). None of them tells you whether the chair fits your body. EN 1335, the European office chair standard, is not legally mandatory for every chair sold in the EU, according to Grandis Trade, yet it functions as the default reference in public tenders and corporate procurement. That gap is why a chair can be called ergonomic without citing a standard.

## What does EN 1335 actually certify?

The table shows what each part of EN 1335 covers, and what a claim to that part leaves unresolved.

| Part | Covers | Leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| EN 1335-1 | Dimensions and measuring methods for types Ax, A, B, C | Comfort and durability |
| EN 1335-2:2018 | Safety, strength, durability requirements | Dimensional fit for a specific user |
| EN 1335-3 | Mechanical test methods for stability, strength, durability | Whether a specific chair passed |

A seller citing part 1 is claiming dimensional conformity, not general comfort. EN 1335-2:2018 carries the load basis: its requirements are calculated around 8 hours of daily use by users weighing up to 110 kg. If you weigh more, or the chair is used across shifts, you are outside the stated basis of that standard. Ask which part a certificate references, because the three parts answer different questions.

The table collects the EN 1335-1 figures that a spec sheet should make comparable.

| EN 1335-1 item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Seat pad width | At least 400 mm |
| Seat pad depth | At least 380 mm |
| Type A adjustment travel | At least 130 mm |
| Seat height measurement | Floor to top of uncompressed cushion |

Adjustment range is not the same as adjustment travel. Two chairs can quote the same seat-height window and land in different types because each type also requires a minimum amount of travel; a chair with the same window but a short travel figure does not meet the higher type. Seat height has a defined measuring method, so if a quote does not state it, the number is not comparable. Seat pad dimensions are minimums, not ranges, and a fixed seat depth rules out Type Ax and Type A. If hip breadth is your fit problem rather than depth, use the separate guide to office chair seat width versus hip breadth because the certification floor is a minimum, not a recommendation.

## What does BIFMA X5.1 test, and what does it leave out?

ANSI/BIFMA X5.1 is a US performance and durability test standard from the Business and Institutional Furniture Manufacturers Association. It is a battery of physical tests: backrest strength, seat drop impact, arm strength, stability, and more. It does not give you a millimetre table. The table collects the details that matter when a seller cites it; the usage basis comes from the standard documentation cited by Bevco.

| X5.1 detail | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| 20 individual tests | Physical tests, not dimensions |
| Ten-year, single-shift basis | Durability usage assumption |
| Section 21 | Out-stop test for adjustable seat depth |

BIFMA itself does not certify products. Manufacturers test voluntarily, and some self-declare that a chair meets BIFMA requirements. Third-party verification exists for products listed in the BIFMA Compliant registry, which are tested at an accredited lab. If a seller says BIFMA tested, ask whether the model is in that registry and which X5.1 sections were performed. A report that cites only a general pass is less useful when the chair has adjustable seat depth, because the dedicated out-stop test is a separate section.

BIFMA G1-2013 is a different document. It is an ergonomics guideline for computer-work furniture, written as fit criteria in words rather than dimensions. A BIFMA ergonomics claim therefore cannot be checked against a numeric specification. A chair can also pass every X5.1 test and still miss an EN 1335-1 type on adjustment travel alone. For a European procurement spec, BIFMA testing answers a durability question and leaves the dimensional one open.

## Where does GREENGUARD fit on a chair spec sheet?

GREENGUARD is a chemical-emissions certification issued against UL 2818, the UL standard for building materials, finishes, and furnishings. It says nothing about structural durability, adjustability, or fit. UL states that GREENGUARD-certified office furniture also complies with BIFMA X7.1, the emissions standard. That is a different document from X5.1, so it does not substitute for durability testing.

The tier matters. GREENGUARD Gold sets lower VOC emission limits than standard GREENGUARD and is positioned for sensitive environments such as nurseries, schools, and healthcare facilities. Gold certification also requires compliance with California Section 01350, the CDPH chamber test method for emissions. If low emissions are your reason for paying attention, ask for Gold and for Section 01350 by name. If structural strength is your concern, the mark is irrelevant.

UL Solutions is the sole owner, operator, and issuer of the GREENGUARD program. That makes the claim checkable against UL's own records rather than a reseller's wording. It also means the mark should be read narrowly: it is a chemical-emissions claim, not an ergonomics credential.

## How do you compare two chairs when both cite certificates?

Start by matching the certificate to the problem. The table shows the common documents and what each one addresses.

| Document or claim | What it addresses | What it leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| ANSI/BIFMA X5.1 | Durability and performance tests | Dimensions and comfort |
| BIFMA G1-2013 | Fit criteria in words | Numeric compliance |
| GREENGUARD | Chemical emissions | Ergonomics and durability |
| GREENGUARD Gold | Lower VOC limits | Structural strength |

Then ask about your own use. A user below 110 kg working one shift is inside the EN 1335-2 basis, so the 110 kg and 8-hour assumptions are not a red flag. A user above 110 kg, or a chair shared across shifts, is outside that basis; look for a manufacturer's stated load and use basis rather than treating a standard citation as a guarantee. A home-office buyer in a small room may care more about GREENGUARD Gold than about X5.1, while a procurement buyer replacing a fleet should care about the BIFMA Compliant listing and the X5.1 sections performed.

Certification is also not a substitute for setup. It will not set your monitor distance or desk height.

## Buying checklist: what to ask before you order

- Ask for the standard number and the part: EN 1335-1, EN 1335-2, or EN 1335-3; X5.1, X7.1, or G1; GREENGUARD or Gold.
- Ask for the test report sections, especially X5.1 Section 21 if the seat depth adjusts.
- Ask how seat height was measured: floor to the top of the uncompressed cushion.
- Ask for both the seat-height range and the travel figure.
- Ask for the load and shift basis. EN 1335-2 uses 110 kg and 8 hours a day; X5.1 uses a ten-year, single-shift assumption.
- Ask for the emissions tier and method: GREENGUARD Gold and CDPH Section 01350 if low VOC matters.
- Check the BIFMA Compliant registry and UL's records rather than relying on a logo.
- Sit in the chair for a working session if you can. A certificate cannot tell you where the seat edge presses behind your knee.

## When the certificate is not enough

Once you have measured your seated height and adjusted what you own, the remaining problems tend to be structural. The table pairs common fit problems with the specification to ask for.

| Situation | What to look for |
|---|---|
| X5.1 pass but fixed seat depth presses behind the knee | Adjustable seat depth |
| Seat-height range fits, but travel is too short to share a desk with another user | Greater height travel or a height-adjustable desk |
| User above 110 kg or multi-shift use | A documented higher load rating and multi-shift test basis |
| Small room and low-VOC concern | GREENGUARD Gold documentation |

A certificate tells you what was tested, not whether the chair fits you; use the table to turn a mismatch into a question for the manufacturer.
