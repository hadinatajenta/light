## Quick answer

For a full-size keyboard and a mouse sharing one surface, plan on a 900 x 400 mm mat: the keyboard accounts for 600 mm of the width before the mouse gets any room, and 400 mm of depth keeps a low-sensitivity mouse sweep on cloth instead of bare desk. Specify rubber-backed cloth 3 mm to 4 mm thick with a stitched perimeter. A rigid pad cannot span this footprint.

The table sets out the figures that decide whether a mat is big enough.

| What | Figure | Why it decides the purchase |
| Mat footprint | 900 x 400 mm | One surface, no seam under either hand |
| Desk width for a full-size board | 600 mm | Committed before the mouse zone starts |
| Mat depth | 400 mm | Keeps the mouse arc on cloth |
| Thickness | 3–4 mm | Rubber-backed cloth, no gel layer |
| Perimeter | Stitched | Heat-sealed and raw-cut edges fray |

A desk pad is one of the simplest fixes in a home office and one of the easiest things to get wrong. The keyboard goes down, the mouse runs out of cloth, and the mat turns out to be a keyboard mat with a decorative border. Nearly every failure is dimensional.

This guide uses manufacturer dimension guides and mat-builder specifications to set the width and depth a keyboard-plus-mouse mat needs, explain why hard pads cannot cover that span, and show how to check a listing before you order.

## How much desk width does a full keyboard and mouse need?

Dareu's guide puts a full-size keyboard at 600 mm of horizontal desk space on its own, before a mouse is added. Corsair's keyboard-size guide is blunter about the consequence: the full-size footprint can be a tight squeeze on smaller desks and takes space that would otherwise go to the mouse.

Run the arithmetic on your own setup. If a 250 mm mouse sweep is a fair description of how you use the mouse, a 600 mm keyboard plus that sweep is 850 mm of working width before edge clearance. That is the case for a 900 x 400 mm mat, the size Kinetic Labs specifies as fitting a full keyboard and a mouse zone deep enough for low-sensitivity sweeps, with no seam under either hand.

Keeping a numeric keypad does not have to cost that width. A 98-key layout measures 380 to 400 mm, only 40 to 60 mm narrower than full-size, while retaining the numpad, arrows and function row. A 75% board saves more space, but Corsair notes the right-hand keys vary between brands, so check the specific layout rather than assuming.

The table compares the three layouts on the numbers that decide how much mat you need.

| Layout | Board width (mm) | Numpad | Trade-off |
| Full-size |  | Yes | 600 mm of desk width before mouse room |
| 98-key | 380 to 400 | Yes | 40 to 60 mm narrower, keeps arrows and function row |
| 75% |  | No | Right-side keys differ by brand |

If you already own a full-size board, size the mat around it: a 900 mm mat still leaves mouse room on top of the 600 mm the keyboard claims, as long as the desk can take the overall footprint. If you are buying board and mat together, a 98-key plus a 900 mm mat buys the most mouse room without losing the numpad.

## How deep should a desk pad be?

Depth is the dimension buyers skip, and the mouse is what exposes it. A mouse arcs forward and back rather than tracking in a straight line, so a pad that ends before the keyboard's front edge sends the mouse onto bare desk mid-sweep. Tracking and feel change at the worst possible moment.

Kinetic Labs sizes its keyboard-plus-mouse mat at 400 mm deep for that reason: the keyboard sits at the back, the mouse gets a forward arc in front of it, and both stay on one surface. Treat 400 mm as the working figure for a full-size board. Measure desk depth before ordering, letting the tape include the monitor base or arm clamp.

## Is a soft mat or a hard pad the right choice?

Cloth, for this span. Corsair's comparison of hard and soft pads puts the practical ceiling for a rigid pad at roughly 40 x 40 cm, because hard pads become more fragile as they grow. That is a mouse-only size and cannot cover a keyboard plus mouse zone without a seam.

Hard pads still earn their place on a mouse-only desk, where the surface wipes clean and does not absorb spills. Once the keyboard has to sit on the same mat, the size ceiling settles the question. Cloth has its own costs: it collects dust and skin oil, and a pale mat shows both quickly. Check the maker's care instructions before washing, since a rubber backing washed the wrong way can warp.

## What thickness and edge finish should you look for?

Thickness is a spec to compare, not a grade. Thekapco builds at 3 mm and notes other current makers range from 2 mm to 4 mm. Kinetic Labs standardises on 4 mm rubber-backed cloth and frames the figure as wrist compliance plus absorption of desk resonance: the mat gives wrists a slightly soft landing instead of the dead stop of a hard pad on wood, and it damps the knock of a keyboard on bare desktop. A thinner mat gives less of that cushioning under the wrist.

The edge is the durability tell. Heat-sealed perimeters (the cut edge melted shut) and raw-cut edges are reported to fray within months once daily mouse friction works at the same corner. Stitched edges are described as holding for years on that corner, which on a wide mat sits directly under your forearm where you will see it every day.

Skip gel-filled wrist pads. In a reported purchase, a cheap gel mouse pad delaminated (the cloth top lifted away from the filling) within about two weeks. That is one account rather than a rate, but a flat mat has no filling to leak.

## What do 'speed' and 'control' mean on a mat listing?

Very little on their own. Thekapco states that these and similar words are comparative descriptions that need a reference product or test method to mean anything. A mat sold as control-surfaced is more controlled than something the listing rarely names. Judge the checkable parts instead: weave, backing, thickness, perimeter construction.

## How do you check the size before you order?

Measure the keyboard at its widest point, add the sweep you actually use, and add clearance at both edges. One worked example: a 440 mm keyboard, a 250 mm sweep and 20 mm of clearance at each edge gives 730 mm, so a 900 mm mat leaves 170 mm spare. Run it backwards for a tight desk. If the gap between a monitor stand and the desk edge is 880 mm, a 900 mm mat will not sit flush.

Then check the tolerance. Thekapco lists an approved overall-dimension variation of roughly 1 to 2 mm, not 1 to 2 cm. That is tight enough for a recessed desk, and it also means ordering to an exact gap is risky.

Read the numbers in the listing rather than the photographs. A reporter comparing a replacement pad with the old one found it about two inches narrower than expected, even though the dimensions were printed on the product page.

## What should you check before you order a desk pad?

- Keyboard width measured at its widest point, including any raised frame or wrist rest.
- Usable desk width and depth measured, minus the monitor base, arm clamp and anything else on the surface.
- Mat width at least keyboard width plus mouse sweep plus 10 to 20 mm of clearance per side.
- 400 mm of depth where the desk allows it, less only if you have measured and accepted a shorter mouse run.
- Stitched perimeter rather than heat-sealed or raw-cut.
- 3 mm to 4 mm rubber-backed cloth, flat, with no gel strip.
- Dimensions in the listing read in millimetres, with a few millimetres of slack for tolerance.
- Care instructions checked so the backing survives a wash.
