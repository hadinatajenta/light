An office chair is the one piece of home office equipment you cannot easily work around. A slow
monitor is annoying and a poor keyboard is tiring, but a bad chair becomes a back and neck problem.
The features that prevent that are not the ones the marketing leads with.

## Adjustability is the whole point

A chair that fits you beats a chair that is merely plush. Look for adjustable seat height, seat
depth, armrest height, and ideally armrest position. Lumbar support should be adjustable in height so
it meets the curve of your lower back rather than resting on it. If a chair cannot be set to your
body, no amount of padding will fix it.

## Seat depth and edge pressure

Seat depth matters more than seat width. You want roughly two to three fingers of space between the
back of your knee and the front edge of the seat. Too deep and the edge presses behind the knee,
reducing circulation. Too shallow and your thighs carry your weight. Adjustable depth solves this,
which is why it is worth paying for.

## Lumbar support done properly

A firm, height-adjustable lumbar pad is more useful than a soft pillow. It should encourage the
natural inward curve of the lower back without pushing you forward. If your chair lacks one, a small
rolled towel can test the effect before you spend money on an accessory.

## The armrests often get in the way

Armrests should support your forearms without lifting your shoulders or stopping you from pulling
close to the desk. If they are fixed and too high, they cause as much harm as good. Adjustable height
is the minimum; adjustable width and depth are better still.

## Materials, build, and repair

Mesh breathes and stays cool, while foam feels softer at first but compresses over time. Check the
weight rating and the warranty, especially on the gas cylinder and the mechanism, which are the parts
that fail. A chair that sells replacement parts is a better long-term buy than one that does not.

## What is worth the premium

Pay for adjustability, a strong mechanism, and a warranty that covers years of use. Do not pay extra
for racing stripes, a brand on the headrest, or a massage feature you will use twice. A used premium
chair in good condition is often a better value than a new budget one.

## Test it like you mean it

Sit for at least ten minutes, adjust everything, and notice whether your feet stay flat and your
shoulders relax. If a shop will not let you, buy from somewhere with a real return policy. Your body
is the only specification that matters here, and it is worth more than any feature list.
