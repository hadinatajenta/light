The Studio Display XDR starts at $3,299, and the first number that matters is not 2,000 nits. It is 47.8cm, the height of the top edge when the included stand is at its lowest. If that lands at your seated eye line, this monitor fixes Apple's most annoying desktop problem. If it doesn't, the best panel Apple has sold for a desk still won't fit yours.

## Quick answer

- **Buy it if** you are Mac-first, your work needs HDR or wide-gamut color, and you want one cable for display, charging, camera, speakers, and ports.
- **The stand is the desk fix.** The XDR includes the height-adjustable stand that costs $400 extra on the standard model.
- **The panel is the upgrade.** 27-inch 5K, 218ppi, 2,304-zone mini-LED, 2,000-nit HDR peaks, 120Hz adaptive.
- **The stand is not an arm.** No swivel, no portrait, and the lowest position is still 47.8cm above the desk.
- **It is Mac-first.** No HDMI, no DisplayPort, no headphone jack, one upstream computer.
- **The standard Studio Display at $1,599 is the better buy for most home offices,** or a $699 ASUS ProArt if you skip HDR.

## What the XDR actually is

Apple [announced](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/03/apple-unveils-new-studio-display-and-all-new-studio-display-xdr) the XDR on March 3, 2026, with a refreshed standard Studio Display. It replaces the 32-inch 6K Pro Display XDR, which cost $4,999 without a stand. The new model is 27 inches, 5120x2880, 218ppi. It [starts at $3,299](https://www.apple.com/shop/buy-mac/studio-display-xdr) with the tilt-and-height stand ($3,299.99 on [PCMag's price sheet](https://www.pcmag.com/reviews/apple-studio-display-xdr)), $3,599 with nano-texture glass, and $2,899 in the VESA configuration. The VESA version is $400 cheaper because you are not buying the stand.

The 27-inch size is the first compromise. If you need 32 inches or 6K, Apple no longer sells it. Third-party 32-inch 6K panels exist, but you give up the camera, speakers, and integration. On a 60cm desk, the [24 vs 27-inch comparison](/blog/24-vs-27-inch-monitor-at-60-cm-on-a-shallow-desk/) covers whether 27 inches is too much. Nano-texture adds $300 and is worth it only if a window or ceiling light reflects in the panel.

## The panel: 5K, mini-LED, 120Hz

5120x2880 on 27 inches is 218 pixels per inch. At 60cm, that is about 90 pixels per degree; at 70cm, about 105. Both are beyond the eye's resolution. Text looks like print, and two browser windows at the default 2560x1440 scaled resolution each get about 1280 points.

The mini-LED backlight has 2,304 dimming zones and a 1,000,000:1 contrast ratio. Apple rates 1,000 nits for SDR and 2,000 nits peak for HDR. The peak is a window, not a full-screen number: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/891436/apple-studio-display-xdr-review) measured 2,000 nits holding to a 25% window, then tapering to just under 1,000 nits at full white.

Color is the other reason to pay. The panel covers P3 and Adobe RGB, with over 80% Rec. 2020 coverage per Apple's announcement. The Verge measured P3 coverage hitting 100% in the modes that support it, and its reference-mode testing found color and grayscale accuracy mostly in the imperceptible range. For a 4K HDR grade, that is enough to trust the saturation and highlights. The weak spot is shadow detail: The Verge found the low end of the grayscale too bright out of the box, so dark scenes lose depth until you calibrate. It is still an LCD, so bright objects on black will halo.

120Hz with Adaptive Sync runs from 47Hz to 120Hz. On a supported Mac, scrolling and video are smoother. 120Hz is not universal: [Apple's spec page](https://www.apple.com/studio-display-xdr/specs/) and [MacRumors' compatibility note](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/03/03/studio-display-xdr-120hz-limits/) put M1 (including M1 Pro, Max, and Ultra), M2, and M3 at 60Hz. Full 120Hz needs an M2 Pro/Max/Ultra, an M3 Pro/Max/Ultra, an M4 Pro/Max, or an M5-series chip. The published list skips the plain M4, so verify your exact chip before ordering.

## The stand and the desk

The XDR includes the stand that Apple charges $400 extra for on the standard model. It gives 105mm (4.1 inches) of height travel and tilts -5 to +25 degrees. The top edge sits 47.8cm above the desk at the bottom and 58.3cm at the top. The footprint is 21.4cm deep. The stand is not removable, so you pick it or the VESA adapter at checkout.

On a 70cm-deep desk, the stand eats 21.4cm and leaves 48.6cm in front. Sit with your eyes 10-15cm from the front edge and you are 60-65cm from the panel, the sweet spot for 27 inches at 218ppi. On a 60cm desk, 38.6cm left is tight with a keyboard. The [desk sizing guide](/blog/how-to-size-a-desk-for-a-monitor-depth-width-and-viewing-distance/) has the depth math.

Height is the harder check. A 74cm desk puts the top edge between 121.8cm and 132.3cm off the floor. If your seated eye line is around 122cm, the low setting puts the top edge at eye level. If your eye line is 118cm, the top edge is still 3.8cm above your eyes at the lowest setting. The Verge's reviewer found the top edge four inches above his eye line at the lowest setting. The stand adjusts, but it does not go low enough for every desk. The [monitor height on an arm guide](/blog/monitor-height-on-an-arm-eye-line-tilt-and-viewing-distance/) explains the eye-line math.

The articulating arm moves the screen slightly farther away at the extremes. There is no swivel or portrait rotation on the stand, so choose the VESA version at checkout if you need either.

The VESA version is $2,899, $400 less than the stand version, and it drops the weight to 6.3kg and the depth to 3.3cm. It supports 100x100mm plates and landscape or portrait. That suits a shallow desk or an existing arm. The [VESA plate guide](/blog/vesa-mount-compatibility-plate-sizes-and-screw-patterns/) covers the screw pattern.

The standard model's height-adjustable stand costs $1,999 all in; the XDR includes it for $3,299. The M6 iMac is tilt-only, so it needs a riser or arm; the [riser versus arm guide](/blog/monitor-riser-vs-arm-when-a-shelf-beats-an-arm/) covers the alternatives.

## Ports and one-cable docking

There are four ports: two Thunderbolt 5 and two USB-C. One Thunderbolt 5 port is upstream to the Mac, with 140W of charging. The other is downstream, up to 120Gb/s, for a second display or fast storage. Two USB-C ports run at 10Gb/s for peripherals and storage. There is no HDMI, DisplayPort, USB-A, or headphone jack.

140W is enough for a 16-inch MacBook Pro. One cable carries the display signal, charges the laptop at full speed, and gives you two USB-C ports on the back of the monitor. With the camera, speakers, and mics, it replaces a dock, webcam, microphone, and desk speakers. That is the strongest argument for the price. The [cable management guide](/blog/cable-management-for-a-small-desk/) has the routing details.

The included 1m cable is short if your Mac sits far away. One upstream port means swapping the cable to switch computers. There is no KVM. The [M6 MacBook Pro 14 desk review](/blog/m6-macbook-pro-14-review-desk-setup/) shows how the single-cable setup behaves.

Windows works for video over Thunderbolt 5 or USB-C, but Apple says resolution and refresh depend on the device, and the camera, brightness keys, and reference modes are Mac-only. If your desk is Windows-first, buy a 5K monitor with HDMI, DisplayPort, and a KVM.

## Camera, speakers, and mics

The 12MP Center Stage camera is a real upgrade over the 2022 model: better low-light processing, less noise, and framing that follows you. It is still a webcam, not an iPhone. The Verge called it clear with minimal noise. For Zoom and Slack, it is fine.

Desk View crops the ultrawide image to show your desk from above, useful for sketching or showing an object on a call. It needs depth: on a shallow desk, expect to see more keyboard than desk. On a 70cm desk with the monitor at the back, it works.

The audio is surprisingly good. Six speakers, four force-cancelling woofers, two tweeters, Spatial Audio. The Verge calls it the best sound it has heard from a computer display. [AppleInsider's review](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/03/27/studio-display-xdr-review-an-almost-pixel-perfect-monitor-for-creative-pros) also praises the audio and the panel uniformity. The three-mic array is fine for calls, not recording. There is no headphone jack, so headphones go into the Mac.

## XDR versus the standard Studio Display

Same size, same resolution, same 218ppi, same camera, same speakers, same port layout. The differences are the panel, the stand, the charging wattage, and the price. The standard model is $1,599 with a tilt-only stand, $1,999 with the height-adjustable stand, and $1,599 with VESA. Nano-texture is $300 on both. The standard panel is 600 nits and 60Hz, with no local dimming and no HDR.

The $400 stand upgrade is annoying. A standard Studio Display with the height-adjustable stand is $1,999, $1,300 less than the XDR. If you do not need HDR or 120Hz, that is the rational Apple buy. [The Verge's review of the standard model](https://www.theverge.com/tech/929015/apple-studio-display-2026) is blunt: it is an old panel with a better camera and faster ports, and the competition is better value.

## Third-party 5K alternatives

The [ASUS ProArt PA27JCV](https://www.asus.com/us/displays-desktops/monitors/proart/proart-display-5k-pa27jcv/) is $699. It has a 500-nit IPS panel, DisplayHDR 500, 99% P3, 95% Adobe RGB, a matte coating, USB-C with 96W, HDMI 2.1, DisplayPort 1.4, a USB hub, a KVM, 130mm of height travel, swivel, and pivot. It has no camera, no speakers, no Thunderbolt 5, no local dimming, and no 120Hz. For spreadsheets, writing, and SDR editing, it is the value pick, and The Verge names it as the quarter-price 5K option.

The [BenQ PD2730S](https://www.benq.com/en-us/monitor/creative-pro/pd2730s/spec.html) is $1,100. It has a 400-nit IPS panel, 2,000:1 contrast, 98% P3, a matte panel, Thunderbolt 4 with 90W, HDMI, a USB-C hub, a KVM, and a height, swivel, and pivot stand. It has no camera. It is the middle ground for Thunderbolt docking and matte.

## Who should buy it

- **You do color-critical Mac work that pays for the monitor.** HDR grading, print prep with Adobe RGB, photography, 3D. The 2,000-nit peaks and 2,304 zones change what you can see in highlights.
- **You want the cleanest possible Mac desk.** One cable, 140W, camera, speakers, mics, two USB-C ports. A 16-inch MacBook Pro on a 70cm desk with this monitor and a wireless keyboard is a two-cable desk.
- **You have a 70cm+ deep desk and a seated eye line in the stand's range.** Or you are buying the $2,899 VESA version and using an arm you already trust.
- **You have an M2 Pro, M4 Pro, or newer Mac.** Without that, you are paying for 120Hz you cannot drive.
- **You were considering a Pro Display XDR and stand.** This is cheaper, smaller, and better except for size and resolution.

## Who should skip it

- **Your desk is Windows-first or dual-PC.** No HDMI, no DisplayPort, no KVM, and one upstream port.
- **You want 32 inches or 6K.** Apple does not sell one anymore. The 27-inch 5K is the whole lineup.
- **You do not need HDR or 120Hz.** The standard Studio Display at $1,599, or the ASUS at $699, does the same daily job for a lot less.
- **Your total desk budget is under $5,000.** At $3,299, the monitor is two-thirds of that before a Mac, desk, chair, or arm. A $1,599 standard display leaves room for the rest of the setup.
- **You need portrait or swivel.** The stand is landscape-only and does not swivel; the VESA version is the only path to portrait.
- **You want a headphone jack or a power button.** Neither is here.

## Verdict

The Studio Display XDR is the best desktop display Apple has ever made, and the first one that solves the height problem out of the box. The panel is excellent and the stand is included. For a Mac-first creative who grades HDR or preps print, it earns its price.

But $3,299 is still $3,299. The standard Studio Display at $1,599 does the same 27-inch 5K job at 60Hz and 600 nits, and the ASUS ProArt at $699 does it with a more flexible stand and no Apple tax. If your work does not need HDR or 120Hz, the XDR is a want, not a need. If you have an older Mac, a Windows PC on the same desk, or a 60cm-deep desk, it is the wrong buy.

Buy it for the panel and the plumbing, not the logo. It makes sense if your work needs HDR or wide-gamut accuracy, your Mac is new enough for 120Hz, and your desk has the depth and height for a 62.3cm-wide, 21.4cm-deep, 8.5kg monitor. If not, save the money. The standard Studio Display or a third-party 5K will leave more room for the rest of the desk.
