A desk setup is easy to buy and hard to get right, because the mistakes only appear once you are
living with it. The goal is not a photograph. It is a working space that still fits you when your
job, your screens, or your body change.

## Plan for the next three years

Ask what will be on the desk later, not just today. A second monitor, a larger laptop, a printer, or
a microphone all need somewhere to live. Leaving empty space is cheaper than replacing furniture.
Decide early whether you want to sit or stand, because that choice fixes the desk height and the
cables.

## Height, fit, and posture first

The desk and chair work as a pair. Your elbows should rest near ninety degrees and your feet should
touch the floor or a footrest. A fixed-height desk only works if your chair can be raised to meet it,
which is why an adjustable chair often solves a problem people blame on the desk. If you might stand
later, a sit-stand frame is cheaper to add at the start than to retrofit.

## Depth beats width for most people

A deep desk keeps a large monitor at a comfortable distance and leaves room for your forearms. Width
is generous but can tempt clutter. Unless you know you need a very wide surface, favour depth and add
a small side table for the printer rather than buying the widest desk in the shop.

## Cable management is a design decision

Decide where power, network, and display cables will run before the furniture arrives. A tray under
the desk, a channel up one leg, and a single power strip keep the floor clear and make cleaning and
moving easy. Velcro ties beat plastic zip ties because you will reopen them.

## Light and sight lines

Place the desk so you are not facing a window at eye level, which causes glare, or with a bright wall
behind you, which turns you into a silhouette on video calls. A monitor arm and a small task light
solve more problems than a bigger screen.

## Buy in the right order

Start with the chair and the desk, because they are expensive to change and affect your body. Then add
the monitor and its arm. Then storage and cable parts. Accessories are the easiest to get wrong, so
buy them last once you can see the real gaps.

## Guard against clutter creep

Give every device a home: a drawer for cables, a shelf for the printer, a hook for headphones. A
setup you cannot tidy in five minutes will not stay tidy. Simplicity here is not minimalism for its
own sake, it is the thing that makes the desk easy to use every day.

Plan for a workspace that can absorb one more screen and one more device without a rebuild, and you
will spend far less over the next few years than someone chasing a new desk every time their job
changes.
