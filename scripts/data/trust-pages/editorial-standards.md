Light Scripts publishes buying guides for desk and home-office gear and the work devices on the desk: chairs, desks, monitors, keyboards, audio, laptops, and tablets. This page explains how a guide is researched, what we claim and do not claim, how AI is used, and how corrections work. It is the standard we hold every guide to.

## Who is responsible

If you find an error, tell us through the [contact page](/contact/) and it will be corrected. ## How we research

We gather evidence before we write.

- We search the live web and fetch the pages we rely on, rather than working from memory.
- We prefer primary sources: standards bodies, government and regulator guidance, manufacturer documentation, and measurements published by independent labs and reviewers.
- When a claim depends on a source, the draft must quote it, and our system checks that the quote actually appears in the page we fetched. Notes that cannot be verified against the fetched text are not used.
- We record the source URL for every note so a reader or an editor can check it.
- When the evidence for an angle is thin, we say so rather than fill the gap with assumptions.

## What kinds of guides we publish

- **Buying guides and explainers** start from a decision, such as desk depth, chair size, or the ports a laptop needs, and work through the standards and measurements that decide it.
- **Review roundups** bring together the measurements and verdicts that independent reviewers and labs published for a new product. Every outlet is named and linked, disagreements between them are reported, and the guide says when a number comes from a summary rather than the original test.
- **Hands-on reviews** are written after the editor has used the product. They say what was tested, how, and for how long, and they show the editor's own photos and measurements next to any figures taken from other sources.

## How we recommend

- We separate manufacturer claims from independently reported findings from our own analysis, and we label which is which.
- We explain who a product suits and who should avoid it, and we make the trade-offs explicit.
- We do not call a product the best overall unless the evidence supports that, and we explain the conditions under which each recommendation makes sense.
- We do not invent specifications, measurements, prices, discounts, availability, warranty terms, ratings, review counts, or test results. When a number cannot be attributed to a source, we describe the relationship instead of stating the number.

## What we do not claim

Unless an article says otherwise, we have not physically tested the products we write about. We do not claim hands-on testing, personal use, or measurements we did not perform. When a guide reports performance, battery life, or a benchmark result, it names the source that measured it.

## AI assistance

Most guides on this site are drafted with AI assistance. The assistant works from pages our system fetched: it organizes research and drafts prose, and it is not allowed to add facts that are not in those sources. A claim that cannot be matched to a source is removed.

Before November 2026, many guides were checked by an automated review step that applies these rules. Since November 2026, every new guide is also approved by the editor before it is published. We disclose this because you deserve to know how an article was made.

## Images

Cover images are either AI-generated illustrations or photos used with credit. Credited photos are manufacturer press images, which we may crop or label, and Creative Commons images used under their stated license; the credit appears under the cover. AI-generated illustrations show a general scene, not the specific product a guide discusses.

## Sources and citations

Articles link the sources they relied on. We cite authoritative material by name where a claim depends on it, and we never invent a study, a test result, or a source we are not certain exists. Where a guide would benefit from a source we could not verify, we prefer to say less rather than guess.

## Updates and corrections

- We revise articles when the underlying products, prices, or standards change.
- When we correct a factual error, we fix it rather than quietly delete it, and the updated date on the article reflects the change.
- If you spot a mistake, email us. Corrections from readers are welcome and taken seriously.
